1.1 Maxwell’s Original Pre-Tensor Component & Quaternion Formulation (1865 / 1873: \(20\) Equations)¶

In A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field (1865) [Maxwell, 1865] and A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism (1873) [Maxwell, 1873], Maxwell did not write four vector equations in \(\mathbf{E}\) and \(\mathbf{B}\). Instead, Maxwell wrote 20 component equations in 20 field variables (maxwellOriginal1865EquationCount = 20 in src/signals/Signals/DFT.lean, lines 7990–7996 and MAXWELL_ORIGINAL_1865_EQUATION_COUNT = 20 in src/signals/numerical_adapter/src/dft_plabic_vrc.rs, lines 2165–2167):

  • \(3\) magnetic induction components from the electrotonic state (magnetic vector potential \(\mathbf{A}\)): \(\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}\),

  • \(3\) electromotive intensity components including convective motion \(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}\) and scalar potential \(\phi\): \(\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B} - \partial_t\mathbf{A} - \nabla\phi\),

  • \(3\) total current components adding Maxwell’s displacement current to conduction current: \(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{tot}} = \mathbf{J} + \partial_t\mathbf{D}\),

  • \(3\) Ampère–Maxwell magnetomotive curl equations: \(\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{tot}}\),

  • \(3\) dielectric elasticity equations: \(\mathbf{D} = \varepsilon\mathbf{E}\),

  • \(3\) Ohm’s conduction equations: \(\mathbf{J} = \sigma_e\mathbf{E}\),
