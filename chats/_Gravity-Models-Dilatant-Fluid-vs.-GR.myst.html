The Dilatant Fluid Model of Gravity vs. Observation¶

The idea that the physical vacuum acts as a shear-thickening (dilatant) fluid—most notably proposed in recent years by researchers like Marco Fedi—attempts to replace the curved spacetime of General Relativity with fluid dynamics. By substituting the viscosity term in Stokes’ law with the Lorentz factor (\(\gamma = 1 / \sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}\)), this model mathematically back-calculates the correct value for the anomalous precession of Mercury’s perihelion.

However, mathematical coincidence in a single kinematic calculation does not make a theory viable. When subjected to the broader scope of astrophysical observation, the dilatant vacuum model severely breaks down.

Here is exactly where the model fails to jive with reality:

  • The Pioneer Anomaly Overcorrection: Proponents of the dilatant fluid model point to the “Pioneer Anomaly” (an unexplained deceleration of the Pioneer 10 and 11 space probes) as proof of viscous drag in the vacuum. However, the Pioneer Anomaly was definitively solved in 2012. Extensive thermal modeling proved that the deceleration was caused entirely by anisotropic thermal radiation—the spacecraft were simply radiating more heat from their radioactive power sources out of their fronts than their backs, acting as a weak photon thruster. If a dilatant vacuum also caused a drag force equal to the anomaly, the probes would have decelerated twice as much as we actually observed.

  • Violation of Lorentz Invariance: A fundamental premise of fluid models is that the fluid itself has a “rest frame.” Moving through a fluid implies an absolute velocity relative to that medium, which directly violates Lorentz invariance and the equivalence principle. Decades of high-precision tests—such as observations of gamma-ray bursts by the Fermi Space Telescope traveling billions of light-years without dispersion—show that Lorentz invariance holds to incredibly strict tolerances. A physical, viscous fluid vacuum would disperse and drag light in a way that is utterly absent from astronomical observation.

  • Energy Dissipation and Orbital Decay: Viscous drag is a non-conservative force; it dissipates kinetic energy into heat. If Mercury is plowing through a shear-thickening fluid, the drag required to shift its perihelion would simultaneously bleed its orbital energy. General Relativity accounts for the perihelion shift as a geometric effect of energy-momentum conservation in curved spacetime, requiring no energy loss other than negligible gravitational wave emission. A fluid drag model would require the planets to eventually spiral into the sun due to viscous dissipation, contradicting the long-term stability of the solar system.

  • The Tensor Nature of Gravity: General Relativity is a rank-2 tensor field theory. This specific mathematical structure is what perfectly predicts the exact angle of light-bending around stars and the precise quadrupole polarization of gravitational waves directly detected by LIGO and Virgo. Fluid dynamics, governed by the Navier-Stokes equations, relies on vector fields. Vector and scalar theories of gravity historically fail to predict the correct light-bending factor (often missing by exactly half) and cannot natively reproduce the quadrupole radiation of black hole mergers.

Is CFD the Viable Way Forward?¶

In short: No, not as a replacement for fundamental physics. Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) is an incredibly powerful tool for engineering and macroscopic systems, but it is not a viable framework for rewriting the fundamental laws of gravity.

That said, fluid dynamics does have a legitimate and exciting place in theoretical physics through Analog Gravity. Physicists use fluid models (like Bose-Einstein condensates or acoustic fluids) to simulate and study kinematics that mimic curved spacetime. For example, “dumb holes” (acoustic black holes) created in labs use fluid flow exceeding the speed of sound to simulate event horizons, allowing researchers to study analogs of Hawking radiation.

However, it is crucial to recognize the distinction: these fluids are simulating the kinematics of spacetime, not causing them. Because fluid models inherently lack general covariance and fail to replicate the dynamic tensor geometry of the Einstein Field Equations, relying on CFD to decode the fundamental nature of gravity is considered a theoretical dead end.
