Glossary¶

Knowledge Representation¶

BFO¶

Basic Formal Ontology — a top-level foundational ontology that provides abstract categories (continuant, occurrent, etc.) used as upper classes by IOF and many domain ontologies.

  • Also known as:

    • basic formal ontology

  • abbreviation: BFO

  • category: Knowledge Representation

  • See also:

    • IOF

    • RDF

IOF¶

Industrial Ontologies Foundry — a consortium of ontology developers and industrial stakeholders that publishes a suite of interoperable foundational ontologies for manufacturing, supply chain, and related domains.

  • Also known as:

    • industrial ontology foundry

    • iof core

  • abbreviation: IOF

  • category: Knowledge Representation

  • See also:

    • BFO

    • RDF

Knowledge Graph¶

A structured representation of entities and the relationships between them, encoded as an RDF graph and queryable with SPARQL.

  • Also known as:

    • knowledge representation

    • semantic model

  • category: Knowledge Representation

  • See also:

    • RDF

    • SPARQL

Ontology¶

A formal, machine-readable specification of the concepts within a domain and the relationships between them. In this project, ontologies are encoded as RDF/OWL and follow the IOF architecture.

  • Also known as:

    • ontology

    • knowledge representation

    • semantic model

  • category: Knowledge Representation

  • See also:

    • IOF

    • RDF

QUDT¶

Quantities, Units, Dimensions, and Types — an ontology suite that provides a machine-readable vocabulary for physical quantities and their units of measure, used to annotate numeric data in the knowledge graph.

  • Also known as:

    • quantities units dimensions types

    • units of measure

    • uom

  • abbreviation: QUDT

  • category: Knowledge Representation

  • See also:

    • RDF

RDF¶

Resource Description Framework — a W3C standard for representing information as subject–predicate–object triples, forming the basis of linked data and knowledge graphs.

  • Also known as:

    • Resource Description Framework

    • semantic web

    • {term}`Linked Data`

  • abbreviation: RDF

  • category: Knowledge Representation

  • See also:

    • Turtle

    • SPARQL

    • Knowledge Graph

RDF-star¶

An extension of RDF that allows triples to be used as the subject or object of another triple, enabling metadata (such as confidence scores or provenance) to be attached directly to individual statements.

RDF-star is now part of RDF 1.2.

  • category: Knowledge Representation

  • See also:

    • RDF

    • Turtle-star

SPARQL¶

SPARQL Protocol and RDF Query Language — the W3C-standard query language for retrieving and manipulating data stored in RDF format.

  • abbreviation: SPARQL

  • category: Knowledge Representation

  • See also:

    • RDF

Materials & Chemistry¶

Biochar¶

A carbon-rich solid produced by pyrolysis of biomass in a low-oxygen environment. Used as a soil amendment, reductant in carbothermic processes, and precursor to activated carbon.

  • Also known as:

    • activated carbon

    • pyrolytic carbon

    • charcoal

  • category: Materials & Chemistry

  • See also:

    • Pyrolysis

    • Lignin

CNT¶

Carbon nanotube — a cylindrical nanostructure of graphitic carbon with exceptional mechanical, thermal, and electrical properties. Both single-walled (SWCNT) and multi-walled (MWCNT) forms are studied as reinforcing agents in polymer composites.

  • Also known as:

    • carbon nanotube

    • carbon nanotubes

    • multi-walled carbon nanotube

    • single-walled carbon nanotube

    • mwcnt

    • swcnt

    • nanotube

  • abbreviation: CNT

  • category: Materials & Chemistry

  • See also:

    • Vitrimer

Lignin¶

A complex aromatic polymer found in plant cell walls and a major by-product of pulping processes. Kraft lignin (from the kraft pulping process) is the most abundant industrial form and is being explored as a renewable carbon source for binders, carbon fibers, and biochar.

  • Also known as:

    • kraft lignin

    • kraft-lignin

    • organosolv lignin

    • soda lignin

  • category: Materials & Chemistry

  • See also:

    • Biochar

    • Pyrolysis

Vitrimer¶

A class of covalent adaptable network (CAN) polymer that behaves like a thermoset at low temperatures but can be reshaped, welded, or recycled above an activation temperature via bond-exchange reactions, offering a route to closed-loop recyclability.

  • Also known as:

    • vitrimer polymer

    • vitrimeric

    • Covalent Adaptable Network

    • CAN polymer

  • category: Materials & Chemistry

  • See also:

    • CNT

Industrial Processes¶

Additive Manufacturing¶

Layer-by-layer fabrication of parts directly from a digital model, including fused deposition modeling (FDM), selective laser sintering (SLS), and other techniques. Enables complex geometries and low-waste production.

  • Also known as:

    • 3d printing

    • Fused Deposition Modeling

    • FDM

    • Selective Laser Sintering

    • SLS

  • category: Industrial Processes

  • See also:

    • Sintering

Carbothermic¶

A high-temperature reduction process in which a carbon source (coke, biochar, or graphite) reacts with a metal oxide to produce the elemental metal or carbide and CO/CO₂ off-gas.

  • Also known as:

    • carbothermal reduction

    • carbothermal

  • category: Industrial Processes

  • See also:

    • Biochar

    • Ultrasonic

Extrusion¶

A continuous forming process in which material is forced through a shaped die. Twin-screw extrusion is widely used for melt-compounding of polymers and composites.

  • Also known as:

    • twin-screw extrusion

    • single-screw extrusion

    • melt processing

  • category: Industrial Processes

Process¶

In the sustainablefactory model, a discrete industrial or manufacturing operation described by input materials, equipment, parameters, outputs, costs, and associated metrics.

  • category: Industrial Processes

  • See also:

    • Knowledge Graph

Pyrolysis¶

Thermal decomposition of organic material at elevated temperatures in the absence of oxygen, converting biomass or polymers into biochar, bio-oil, and syngas.

  • Also known as:

    • thermal decomposition

    • thermolysis

    • thermal cracking

  • category: Industrial Processes

  • See also:

    • Biochar

    • Lignin

Sintering¶

A powder consolidation process that bonds particles by heating below the melting point. Spark plasma sintering (SPS) applies pulsed DC current for rapid densification.

  • Also known as:

    • solid-state sintering

    • Spark Plasma Sintering

    • SPS

  • category: Industrial Processes

  • See also:

    • Additive Manufacturing

Ultrasonic¶

Processing that uses high-frequency (> 20 kHz) acoustic waves. In ultrasonication (sonochemistry), cavitation micro-bubbles are exploited for dispersion, surface activation, or driving chemical reactions.

  • Also known as:

    • ultrasonication

    • sonochemical

    • sonication

    • acoustic cavitation

  • category: Industrial Processes

  • See also:

    • Carbothermic

The degree to which materials and energy flows in an industrial system are recovered and re-used rather than discharged as waste, as measured by material circularity indicators and closed-loop recycling rates.

Global Warming Potential — the climate impact of a product or process expressed in CO₂-equivalent (CO₂eq) mass over a 100-year horizon, as quantified in a life cycle assessment.

Our work here (with the “Turner models” etc.) gratefully extends from the Amplituhedron to additional applied domains of particle simulation.

FWIU, Amplituhedron is integrated with B-model string theories, and our work with Witten Twistor string theory and Cachazo’s \(\mathrm{SU}(4)\)-based models (CSW, BCFW, Cachazo–Skinner, CHY) is unified via \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4) \times \mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)^\vee\) (docs/grassmannian-su4-witten-twistor-string-cachazo-ddf-turner-ads-cft-unification-paper.md).

Bohmian blowup¶

The blowup of \(Q = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\) and \(V = j/\rho\) as \(\rho \to 0\) in de Broglie-Bohm theory ([Hirschfelder et al., 1974, Hirschfelder and Tang, 1976, Philippidis et al., 1979, Lopreore and Wyatt, 1999]), which causes nodal singularities and numerical instability in quantum trajectory simulation, alongside the vanishing of phase-gradient velocity \(v_S = 0\) in stationary evanescent barriers ([Sharoglazova et al., 2025]). Resolved in our Three-Channel Plücker–Madelung–QFIM and Dual-iGPE trajectory model (docs/grassmannian-quantum-trajectories-bohmian-weak-nonlinear-optics-paper.md).

SU(4)¶

Special Unitary group of degree 4 (the Lie group of 4x4 matrices with determinant 1)

SU(4) has a real dimension of 15 (n^2-1).

SU(4) is locally isomorphic to spin group Spin(6).

SU(4) is the R-symmetry group in 4D N=4 supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory.

  • wikipedia:

    • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_unitary_group#SU(4)
