1. Polaritons on \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\): Hopfield Bogoliubov Mixing, Vacuum Rabi Anticrossing, and Lyddane–Sachs–Teller Reststrahlen Dispersion¶

When an electromagnetic mode of frequency \(\omega_{\mathrm{ph}} > 0\) couples strongly to a dipolar matter excitation of frequency \(\omega_{\mathrm{ex}} > 0\)—such as a semiconductor Wannier–Mott or Rydberg exciton in a microcavity (Hopfield 1958; Weisbuch et al. 1992; Provenance Chat Log: Rydberg-Polariton Analog Gravity Simulator (2026), lines 1021–1135) or a transverse optical (TO) phonon in a polar dielectric (Huang 1951; Provenance Chat Log: Bulk Polariton Coupling in Lignin-Derived Carbon Fibers (2026), lines 1940–1965)—the normal modesHybridize into Upper Polariton (UP) and Lower Polariton (LP) quasiparticles.

1.1 Vacuum Rabi Splitting, Avoided Crossing, and Exact Inverse Rabi Spectroscopy¶

In Signals.Scattering (src/signals/Signals/Scattering.lean), for photon–matter detuning \(\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}} = \omega_{\mathrm{ph}} - \omega_{\mathrm{ex}}\) and vacuum Rabi coupling strength \(g > 0\), the \(2 \times 2\) Hopfield Hamiltonian

\[\begin{split} H_{\mathrm{Hopfield}} = \begin{pmatrix} \omega_{\mathrm{ph}} & g \\ g & \omega_{\mathrm{ex}} \end{pmatrix} \end{split}\]

has effective Rabi splitting (polaritonEffectiveRabiSplitting) and upper/lower polariton eigenfrequencies (upperPolaritonFreq, lowerPolaritonFreq):

\[ \Omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}}, g) = \sqrt{\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}}^2 + 4 g^2} \ge 2 g > 0, \qquad \omega_{\mathrm{UP},\mathrm{LP}} = \frac{\omega_{\mathrm{ph}} + \omega_{\mathrm{ex}} \pm \Omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}}, g)}{2}. \]

In polariton_hopfield_rabi_and_lst_identities, we prove that the trace and determinant of \(H_{\mathrm{Hopfield}}\) are exact spectral invariants:

\[ \omega_{\mathrm{UP}} + \omega_{\mathrm{LP}} = \omega_{\mathrm{ph}} + \omega_{\mathrm{ex}}, \qquad \omega_{\mathrm{UP}} - \omega_{\mathrm{LP}} = \Omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}}, g) \ge 2 g > 0, \]
\[ \operatorname{reconstructPolaritonRabiCouplingSq}(\omega_{\mathrm{ph}}, \omega_{\mathrm{ex}}, \omega_{\mathrm{UP}}, \omega_{\mathrm{LP}}) = \omega_{\mathrm{ph}}\,\omega_{\mathrm{ex}} - \omega_{\mathrm{UP}}\,\omega_{\mathrm{LP}} = g^2. \]

1.2 Hopfield Photon/Matter Fractions as \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker Channel Weights¶

Defining the normalized Hopfield barycentric photon–matter shift (hopfieldBarycentricShift):

\[ d_{\mathrm{Hopfield}}(\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}}, g) = \frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}}}{\sqrt{\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}}^2 + 4 g^2}} \in (-1, 1), \]

the upper-branch Hopfield photon fraction \(|C|^2\) (hopfieldPhotonWeight) and matter fraction \(|X|^2\) (hopfieldMatterWeight) are:

\[ |C|^2 = \frac{1 + d_{\mathrm{Hopfield}}}{2}, \qquad |X|^2 = \frac{1 - d_{\mathrm{Hopfield}}}{2}. \]

When embedded into the complex Grassmannian \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) via \(M_{\mathrm{Hopfield}} = \operatorname{ddfThermalGr24Matrix}(d_{\mathrm{Hopfield}}, \theta)\), the uncrossed Plücker channel amplitudes satisfy \(\frac{1}{2}|w_A^{\mathbb{C}}|^2 = |C|^2\) and \(\frac{1}{2}|w_B^{\mathbb{C}}|^2 = |X|^2\), yielding the exact Pythagorean Hopfield–Plücker complementarity law (polariton_hopfield_rabi_and_lst_identities):

\[ |C|^2 + |X|^2 = 1, \qquad |C|^2 - |X|^2 = d_{\mathrm{Hopfield}}, \qquad |w_A^{\mathbb{C}}|^2\,|w_B^{\mathbb{C}}|^2 = 4\,|C|^2\,|X|^2 = 1 - d_{\mathrm{Hopfield}}^2 = \frac{4 g^2}{\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}}^2 + 4 g^2} > 0. \]

At zero detuning (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{pol}} = 0\), \(\omega_{\mathrm{ph}} = \omega_{\mathrm{ex}}\)), \(d_{\mathrm{Hopfield}} = 0\), \(|C|^2 = |X|^2 = 1/2\), and the \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker hybridization product reaches its unit maximum \(4|C|^2|X|^2 = 1\) (maximally entangled half-light / half-matter polariton).

1.3 Lyddane–Sachs–Teller (LST) Phonon-Polariton Permittivity, ENZ Longitudinal Modes, & the Reststrahlen Band¶

In polar crystals and structured dielectric composites (\(\text{SiO}_2\) quartzite, \(\text{SiC}\), \(\text{ZnS}\), or Lignin-Vitrimer matrices; Lyddane, Sachs, and Teller 1941; Provenance Chat Log: Classical Near-Fields vs. Proca & ENZ Polaritons (2026), lines 78–115; Provenance Chat Log: IQ-Sampling & ENZ Volume Plasmon Polaritons (2026), lines 15120–15130), coupling between transverse photons and optical phonons with transverse frequency \(\omega_{\mathrm{TO}} > 0\) and longitudinal frequency \(\omega_{\mathrm{LO}} > \omega_{\mathrm{TO}}\) yields the dispersive permittivity (lyddaneSachsTellerPermittivity):

\[ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{pol}}(\omega) = \varepsilon_\infty\,\frac{\omega^2 - \omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2}{\omega^2 - \omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2}. \]

Theorem polariton_hopfield_rabi_and_lst_identities proves three fundamental regimes:

  1. Static Dielectric Limit (\(\omega = 0\)): \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pol}}(0) = \varepsilon_\infty\,\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2}{\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{st}} > \varepsilon_\infty\),

  2. Epsilon-Near-Zero (ENZ) Longitudinal Bulk Polariton Cutoff (\(\omega = \omega_{\mathrm{LO}}\)): \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pol}}(\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}) = 0\), where \(\nabla \cdot (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pol}}\mathbf{E}) = 0\) admits a non-trivial longitudinal bulk polariton wave (\(\mathbf{E} \parallel \mathbf{k}\)) at the longitudinal optical frequency \(\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}\),

  3. Reststrahlen Stop-Band & Surface-Polariton Window (\(\omega_{\mathrm{TO}} < \omega < \omega_{\mathrm{LO}}\)): \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pol}}(\omega) < 0\), where bulk transverse propagation is forbidden and the negative-permittivity dielectric interface supports Surface Phonon Polaritons (SPhPs) by the exact same boundary equations as metallic Surface Plasmon Polaritons.

2. Surface Plasmon Polaritons (SPPs) Without Splats: Continuum Dispersion, \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker Skin-Depth Duality, and Spatial Semigroup¶

A Surface Plasmon Polariton (SPP) (Raether 1988; Barnes, Dereux, and Ebbesen 2003; Maier 2007) is a transverse-magnetic ™ electromagnetic surface wave coupled to collective surface electron oscillations at the planar interface (\(z = 0\)) between a positive-permittivity dielectric (\(\varepsilon_d > 0\) for \(z > 0\)) and a negative-permittivity conductor (\(\varepsilon_m = -|\varepsilon_m| < -\varepsilon_d < 0\) for \(z < 0\), so \(0 < \varepsilon_d < |\varepsilon_m|\)).

2.1 Sub-Wavelength Momentum Enhancement Outside the Light Cone & Evanescent Decay Ratio¶

Solving Maxwell’s TM continuity conditions for \(E_x\) and \(D_z = \varepsilon E_z\) across \(z = 0\) yields the squared effective SPP refractive index (sppEffectiveIndexSq in src/signals/Signals/DDF.lean):

\[ n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2(\varepsilon_d, |\varepsilon_m|) = \left(\frac{c\,k_{\mathrm{SPP}}}{\omega}\right)^2 = \frac{\varepsilon_d\,\varepsilon_m}{\varepsilon_d + \varepsilon_m} = \frac{\varepsilon_d\,|\varepsilon_m|}{|\varepsilon_m| - \varepsilon_d}. \]

In MovingSheath.sppContinuum_gr24_and_semigroup_identities, we prove (with zero splats):

  1. Strict Momentum Enhancement Beyond the Dielectric Light Cone:

    \[ n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2(\varepsilon_d, |\varepsilon_m|) - \varepsilon_d = \frac{\varepsilon_d^2}{|\varepsilon_m| - \varepsilon_d} > 0 \implies k_{\mathrm{SPP}} = k_0\,n_{\mathrm{SPP}} > k_0\sqrt{\varepsilon_d}. \]

    Because \(k_{\mathrm{SPP}}\) strictly exceeds the maximum tangential wavevector \(k_0\sqrt{\varepsilon_d}\sin\theta_{\mathrm{inc}}\) of any propagating plane wave in the dielectric, free-space light cannot directly launch SPPs on a flat interface without momentum-matching via a Kretschmann/Otto prism, a periodic diffraction grating, a high-NA radially polarized focus, or a phase-matched cylindrical microtube (CylindricalSPPMode in src/signals/Signals/ActiveOptics.lean).

  2. Exact Normal Evanescent Skin-Depth Ratio: Normal to the interface, the field decays evanescently as \(\exp(-\kappa_d z)\) in the dielectric (\(z > 0\)) and \(\exp(+\kappa_m z)\) in the conductor (\(z < 0\)), where \(\kappa_d^2 = k_0^2(n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2 - \varepsilon_d)\) and \(\kappa_m^2 = k_0^2(n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2 + |\varepsilon_m|)\). We prove the exact identity:

    \[ \frac{\kappa_d^2}{\kappa_m^2} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2 - \varepsilon_d}{n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2 + |\varepsilon_m|} = \frac{\varepsilon_d^2}{|\varepsilon_m|^2} \implies \frac{\delta_d}{\delta_m} = \frac{\kappa_m}{\kappa_d} = \frac{|\varepsilon_m|}{\varepsilon_d} > 1, \]

    confirming that the evanescent tail extends \(|\varepsilon_m|/\varepsilon_d\) times farther into the dielectric than into the conductor.

2.2 Splat-Free \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker Skin-Depth Duality & Spatial Semigroup¶

Defining the dimensionless SPP permittivity contrast (sppPermittivityContrast):

7.1 The Four-Step Physical & Mathematical Mechanism of Second-Harmonic Resonant Optical Parametric Amplification (OPA)¶

  1. Symmetry Selection Rule — Why \(\chi^{(2)}\) Requires Non-Centrosymmetric Crystals (secondOrderPolarization): The second-order nonlinear polarization is \(P_i^{(2)} = \varepsilon_0 \sum_{jk} \chi_{ijk}^{(2)} E_j E_k\) (secondOrderPolarization χ2 E = χ2 * E^2). Under spatial inversion \(\mathbf{r} \mapsto -\mathbf{r}\), the polar vectors \(\mathbf{P}^{(2)}\) and \(\mathbf{E}\) invert (\(\mathbf{P}^{(2)} \mapsto -\mathbf{P}^{(2)}\), \(\mathbf{E} \mapsto -\mathbf{E}\)), so \(E^2 = (-E)^2\) is invariant while \(P^{(2)}\) changes sign. In any centrosymmetric material, the crystal is invariant under inversion (\(\chi^{(2)} = -\chi^{(2)}\)), which we prove in second_harmonic_opa_and_centrosymmetry_identities forces:

    \[ \chi^{(2)} = -\chi^{(2)} \implies \chi^{(2)} = 0 \implies P^{(2)}(\chi^{(2)}, E) = 0. \]

    Therefore, centrosymmetric pristine graphene, achiral carbon nanotubes, and isotropic amorphous lignin have vanishing bulk \(\chi^{(2)} = 0\). Achieving \(\chi^{(2)}\) second-harmonic generation (SHG) and optical parametric amplification (OPA) requires a non-centrosymmetric crystal lattice—such as Periodically Poled Thin-Film Lithium Niobate (\(\text{TFLN}\), \(\text{LiNbO}_3\), used in Dean et al. 2025), trigonal \(\alpha\)-quartz, or macroscopically aligned chiral Cellulose Nanocrystals (CNCs) (CelluloseStripResonator; Provenance Chat Log: Breaking Centrosymmetry with Aligned CNCs in SHG Resonant OPA (2026), lines 3020–3165).

  2. Resonant Second-Harmonic Recirculation Buildup (shgResonantIntracavityPower): In a conventional single-pass \(\chi^{(2)}\) OPA, an input fundamental wave (\(\omega_0\)) first generates a second-harmonic pump (\(\omega_{\mathrm{SH}} = 2\omega_0\), SecondHarmonicResonance), which then parametrically amplifies a signal (\(\omega_s\)) and idler (\(\omega_i = 2\omega_0 - \omega_s\)) via three-wave mixing \(2\omega_0 \to \omega_s + \omega_i\). Because single-pass SHG is inefficient at low power, conventional OPAs require watt-to-kilowatt peak pump powers. In Dean et al. (2025) (10.1038/s41586-025-09959-z), the integrated waveguide resonator resonantly traps and recirculates the second harmonic (\(2\omega_0\)) with cavity buildup factor \(F_{\mathrm{SH}} \ge 1\) (shgResonantIntracavityPower F_sh P_2w = F_sh * P_2w). We prove in second_harmonic_opa_and_centrosymmetry_identities:

    \[ P_{2\omega} \le P_{2\omega}^{\mathrm{intracav}} = F_{\mathrm{SH}}\,P_{2\omega}, \qquad F_{\mathrm{SH}}\,\left(\frac{P_{\mathrm{thresh}}}{F_{\mathrm{SH}}}\right) = P_{\mathrm{thresh}}, \]

    proving that resonant recirculation lowers the external input power required to reach a parametric threshold \(P_{\mathrm{thresh}}\) by a factor of \(F_{\mathrm{SH}}\)—enabling \(>17\text{ dB}\) broadband gain with \(<200\text{ mW}\) input power (LowPowerIntegratedOPA), while strictly obeying the thermodynamic energy conservation bound \(P_{\mathrm{out}} \le P_{\mathrm{in}} + P_{\mathrm{pump}}\) (ActiveOpticalAmplifier.output_le_input_and_pump).

  3. Phase-Matching Detuning Gate (opaNetGainSq): For parametric coupling rate \(g_{\mathrm{OPA}}\) and wavevector phase mismatch \(\Delta k = k_{2\omega} - k_s - k_i\), the net squared spatial gain rate is (opaNetGainSq):

    \[ g_{\mathrm{net}}^2(g_{\mathrm{OPA}}, \Delta k) = g_{\mathrm{OPA}}^2 - \left(\frac{\Delta k}{2}\right)^2 \le g_{\mathrm{OPA}}^2. \]

    At perfect phase matching (\(\Delta k = 0\)), \(g_{\mathrm{net}}^2(g_{\mathrm{OPA}}, 0) = g_{\mathrm{OPA}}^2\) reaches maximum gain; when piezoelectric or electro-optic detuning shifts \(\Delta k \to 2 g_{\mathrm{OPA}}\), \(g_{\mathrm{net}}^2(g_{\mathrm{OPA}}, 2 g_{\mathrm{OPA}}) = 0\), switching off the parametric gain cleanly without mechanical moving parts.

  4. Symplectic Phase-Sensitive Quadrature Squeezing & Bohm Quantum-Potential Barrier (With and Without Splats: opaSqueezed_splat_and_continuum_identities): In a degenerate phase-sensitive OPA with squeeze parameter \(r_{\mathrm{sq}} = g_{\mathrm{eff}} L \ge 0\), the in-phase quadrature is amplified by \(G_+(r_{\mathrm{sq}}) = \exp(2 r_{\mathrm{sq}}) \ge 1\) (opaAmplifiedQuadratureGain) while the orthogonal quadrature is squeezed by \(G_-(r_{\mathrm{sq}}) = \exp(-2 r_{\mathrm{sq}}) \le 1\) (opaSqueezedQuadratureGain), with exact symplectic unit product \(G_+(r_{\mathrm{sq}})\,G_-(r_{\mathrm{sq}}) = 1\).

    • Without Splats: The squeezed-quadrature continuum Bohm potential scale continuumBohmPotentialMode m hbar (κ0 * exp r_sq) is amplified by \(\exp(2 r_{\mathrm{sq}}) \ge 1\).

    • With Normed Tensor Gaussian Splats (opaSqueezedTensorSplat): A wavepacket with principal widths \(\sigma_1 = \sigma_0 e^{r_{\mathrm{sq}}}\) and \(\sigma_2 = \sigma_0 e^{-r_{\mathrm{sq}}}\) preserves its symplectic phase-space area \(\det\Sigma_{\mathrm{OPA}} = \sigma_0^4\) and peak normalization \(\mathcal{N}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{OPA}}) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_0^2}\) identically (matching our Anosov symplectic invariance theorem anosov_plucker_invariance), while its repulsive core Bohm quantum potential increases to:

      \[ Q_{\mathrm{core}}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{OPA}}) = \frac{\hbar^2\,\bigl(\exp(2 r_{\mathrm{sq}}) + \exp(-2 r_{\mathrm{sq}})\bigr)}{4 m\,\sigma_0^2} \ge \frac{\hbar^2}{2 m\,\sigma_0^2}. \]
