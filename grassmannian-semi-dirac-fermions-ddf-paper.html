Abstract¶

Semi-Dirac fermions are exotic two-dimensional relativistic-nonrelativistic hybrid quasiparticles that behave as massive Schrödinger particles along one spatial axis (\(x\)) (\(E \propto \frac{\hbar^2 k_x^2}{2 m_x^*}\) with effective mass \(m_x^* > 0\)) while simultaneously propagating as massless ultra-relativistic Dirac particles along the orthogonal axis (\(y\)) (\(E \propto \hbar v_y |k_y|\) with zero rest mass \(m_y^* = 0\)) (Dietl, Piéchon & Montambaux 2008; Banerjee, Singh, Pardo & Pickett 2009; Montambaux, Piéchon, Fuchs & Goerbig 2009). Following their landmark magneto-optical observation at the nodal-line crossings of \(\mathrm{ZrSiS}\) by Shao et al. (2024) (Physical Review X 14, 041057, arXiv:2311.03735)—verified via the signature \(B^{2/3}\) Landau-level scaling \(E_n(B) \propto [(n + 1/2)B]^{2/3}\)—a foundational question arises across condensed-matter physics, complex projective geometry, and quantum hydrodynamics:

How are semi-Dirac fermions formulated with and without the Complex Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}\) (\(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,2)\), \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\), and \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,6)\)) and with and without the Dilatant Dark Fluid (DDF) (Signals.DDF), why does a quasiparticle acquire mass in only one direction from a hydrodynamic perspective, and what engineering and quantum-computing applications follow?

In this paper, we formalize and machine-check all four regimes (\(2 \times 2\): without/with \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}\) \(\times\) without/with DDF) in Lean 4 (Signals.DFT Section 19, Signals.Pending), Rust/WASM (dft_plabic_vrc.rs), and Python (SemiDiracAndSU4WittenUnificationEngine):

  1. Regime 1 — Without \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}\), Without DDF (Euclidean 2-Band Bloch Space & \(\mathrm{ZrSiS}\) \(B^{2/3}\) Landau Scaling): We formalize the universal Montambaux–Banerjee–Shao squared dispersion semiDiracEnergySq

    \[ E_{\mathrm{SD}}^2(k_x, k_y; m_x^*, v_y, \Delta_m) = \left(\frac{\hbar^2 k_x^2}{2 m_x^*} + \Delta_m\right)^2 + (\hbar v_y k_y)^2, \]

    proving the transition across the three merging phases (\(\Delta_m < 0\) two-Dirac-cone semimetal with nodes at \(\frac{\hbar^2 k_x^2}{2 m_x^*} = -\Delta_m\); \(\Delta_m = 0\) critical semi-Dirac point with massive \(x\)-branch \(E = \frac{\hbar^2 k_x^2}{2 m_x^*}\) and massless \(y\)-branch \(E = \hbar v_y |k_y|\); and \(\Delta_m > 0\) gapped insulator with gap \(2\Delta_m\)), directional group velocities (semiDiracGroupVelocityX, semiDiracGroupVelocityY), and the exact \(\mathrm{ZrSiS}\) \(B^{2/3}\) Landau-level cube law (semiDiracLandauLevelCube: scaling \(B \mapsto 8 B\) scales \(E_n(B)^3\) by \(8^2 = 64 = (8^{2/3})^3\)).

  2. Regime 2 — With Complex Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}\) (\(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,6)\)), Without DDF: While a single 2-band Bloch spinor lives on \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,2) \cong \mathbb{CP}^1\), capturing the pair of merging valleys \((\mathbf{K}_+, \mathbf{K}_-)\) without branch-cut singularities lifts the state to the canonical affine chart \(Z_{\mathrm{SD}} = (I_2 \mid W_{\mathrm{SD}}) \in \mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4) \cong \mathcal{Q}_4 \subset \mathbb{P}^5(\mathbb{C})\). We prove that the Plücker coordinate \(p_{23}(Z_{\mathrm{SD}}) = \det W_{\mathrm{SD}}\) (semiDiracPluckerDet) equals \(E_{\mathrm{SD}}^2(k_x, k_y; \Delta_m)\) identically, and that the \(\mathrm{SU}(4)\)-invariant Fubini–Study Kähler determinant factor (semiDiracKahlerDetFactor) factors into an exact square:

    \[ \det\bigl(I_2 + W_{\mathrm{SD}} W_{\mathrm{SD}}^\dagger\bigr) = \bigl\|p(Z_{\mathrm{SD}})\bigr\|_{\mathbb{C}^6}^2 = 1 + 2 E_{\mathrm{SD}}^2 + E_{\mathrm{SD}}^4 = \bigl(1 + E_{\mathrm{SD}}^2(k_x, k_y; \Delta_m)\bigr)^2 \ge 1. \]

    In uniaxial-strained rhombohedral trilayer graphene (RTG, \(N=3\)), the 6-orbital space \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,6)\) (\(\dim_{\mathbb{R}} = 16\), \(15\) Plücker coordinates) hosts trigonal-warping pocket merging into a semi-Dirac saddle point.

  3. Regime 3 — Without \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}\), With Dilatant Dark Fluid (DDF Longitudinal Sheath Jamming): DDF (MovingSheath) provides a concrete microscopic hydrodynamic mechanism for why mass exists in only one direction: a bosonic sheath moving or compressed along the \(x\)-axis undergoes longitudinal shear-thickening jamming \(\Lambda_\parallel(\beta_x) = \gamma_\varphi(\beta_x) = (1 - \beta_x^2)^{-1/2} > 1\), generating an enhanced longitudinal effective mass (semiDiracDdfJammedMassX)

    \[ m_x^*(\beta_x) = m_0\,\gamma_\varphi(\beta_x)^2 = \frac{m_0}{1 - \beta_x^2} > m_0 \qquad (\text{e.g., } m_x^*(3/5) = \frac{25}{16} m_0), \]

    while orthogonal transverse shear fluctuations along \(y\) propagate at the gapless superfluid phonon speed \(v_y = c_\phi = (\varrho_{\max}^\varphi j_{\min}^\varphi)^{-1/2}\) with strictly zero rest mass (\(m_y^* = 0\)).

  4. Regime 4 — Unified With Both \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) AND DDF + Anisotropic 2D Tensor Gaussian Splats: Combining the \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker chart with DDF longitudinal sheath jamming (semiDiracDdfGr24EnergySq) and evolving a 2D NormedTensorGaussianSplat2D wavepacket (semiDiracAnisotropicSplatVarX, semiDiracAnisotropicSplatVarY) proves that a semi-Dirac wavepacket undergoes dispersive Bohmian quantum-potential spreading along the massive \(x\)-axis (\(\sigma_x^2(t) = \sigma_{x,0}^2 + \frac{\hbar^2 t^2}{4 (m_x^*)^2 \sigma_{x,0}^2} > \sigma_{x,0}^2\)) while maintaining dispersionless soliton-like constant width along the massless \(y\)-axis (\(\sigma_y^2(t) = \sigma_{y,0}^2\)).

1.3 Why Semi-Dirac Landau Levels Scale as \(B^{2/3}\) (semiDiracLandauLevelCube)¶

In a perpendicular magnetic field \(\mathbf{B} = B\hat{z}\) inside the Landau gauge \(\mathbf{A} = (0, B x, 0)\), minimal Peierls substitution \(k_x \mapsto -i\partial_x\) and \(k_y \mapsto k_y + \frac{e B}{\hbar}x\) transforms \(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{SD}}^2\) (at \(\Delta_m = 0\)) into a quartic anharmonic oscillator in the shifted coordinate \(\tilde{x} = x + \frac{\hbar k_y}{e B}\):

\[ \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{SD}}^2\,\psi_n(\tilde{x}) = \left[\frac{\hbar^4}{4 (m_x^*)^2}\frac{d^4}{d\tilde{x}^4} + e^2 v_y^2 B^2 \tilde{x}^2\right]\psi_n(\tilde{x}) = E_n(B)^2\,\psi_n(\tilde{x}). \]

Rescaling \(\tilde{x} = \ell_{\mathrm{SD}}\,\xi\) with characteristic semi-Dirac magnetic length \(\ell_{\mathrm{SD}} = \bigl(\frac{\hbar^2}{2 m_x^* e v_y B}\bigr)^{1/3} \propto B^{-1/3}\) factors all parameter dependence out of the differential operator, yielding the exact \(\mathbf{B^{2/3}}\) quantization law (Montambaux et al. 2009; Shao et al. 2024):

\[ E_n(B) = \pm\,\alpha_{\mathrm{WKB}}\left(\frac{\hbar^2 e^2 v_y^2}{m_x^*}\right)^{1/3}\left[\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right) B\right]^{2/3} \iff E_n(B)^3 = C_{\mathrm{LL}}^3\,\frac{v_y^2\,\bigl((n + 1/2) B\bigr)^2}{m_x^*}, \]

where \(\alpha_{\mathrm{WKB}} = \bigl[\frac{3\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\Gamma(3/4)}{\Gamma(1/4)}\bigr]^{2/3} \approx 1.1732\). In Lean 4 (semiDiracLandauLevelCube), we prove that scaling \(B \mapsto 8 B\) scales \(E_n(B)^3\) by \(8^2 = 64 = (8^{2/3})^3\), so \(E_n(8B) = 4\,E_n(B)\) (whereas a Schrödinger fermion scales by \(8\times\) and a graphene Dirac fermion scales by \(\sqrt{8} \approx 2.828\times\)).

2.1 Regime 2 in Detail: Why \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,6)\) Are the Natural Manifolds for Dirac-Cone Merging¶

  1. Limitation of \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,2) \cong \mathbb{CP}^1\) Without Higher Grassmannians: For a single isolated Dirac cone, the occupied valence-band eigenstate \(|u_-(\mathbf{k})\rangle \in \mathbb{C}^2\) defines a complex line in \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,2) \cong \mathbb{CP}^1 \cong S^2\) carrying Berry phase \(\Phi_B = \pm\pi\). However, at the semi-Dirac transition (\(\Delta_m \to 0\)), two valleys \((\mathbf{K}_+, \mathbf{K}_-)\) of opposite chirality coalesce and exchange spectral weight across the pseudospin-orbit-coupled 4-component valley-sublattice space \(\mathbb{C}^4 = \operatorname{span}\{|A, K_+\rangle, |B, K_+\rangle, |A, K_-\rangle, |B, K_-\rangle\}\).

  2. Canonical Affine Chart \(Z_{\mathrm{SD}} = (I_2 \mid W_{\mathrm{SD}}) \in \mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\): Selecting the 2-dimensional occupied valence subspace inside the 4-dimensional two-valley space \(\mathbb{C}^4\) defines a point in the complex Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4) \cong \mathcal{Q}_4 \subset \mathbb{P}^5(\mathbb{C})\) (real dimension \(2\times 2\times(4-2) = 8\), \(\binom{4}{2} = 6\) Plücker coordinates). On the canonical affine chart \(Z_{\mathrm{SD}} = (I_2 \mid W_{\mathrm{SD}})\) with

    \[\begin{split} W_{\mathrm{SD}}(T_x, \Delta_m, P_y) = \begin{pmatrix} T_x + \Delta_m & -i P_y \\ i P_y & T_x + \Delta_m \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{C}^{2 \times 2}, \qquad T_x = \frac{\hbar^2 k_x^2}{2 m_x^*},\quad P_y = \hbar v_y k_y, \end{split}\]

    the six homogeneous Plücker coordinates \(p(Z_{\mathrm{SD}}) = (p_{01}, p_{02}, p_{03}, p_{12}, p_{13}, p_{23}) \in \mathbb{C}^6\) are:

    \[ p_{01} = 1, \quad p_{02} = -i P_y, \quad p_{03} = -(T_x + \Delta_m), \quad p_{12} = T_x + \Delta_m, \quad p_{13} = -i P_y, \quad p_{23} = \det W_{\mathrm{SD}} = (T_x + \Delta_m)^2 + P_y^2. \]

    • Exact Klein Quadric Satisfaction:

      \[ p_{01} p_{23} - p_{02} p_{13} + p_{03} p_{12} = 1\cdot\bigl((T_x + \Delta_m)^2 + P_y^2\bigr) - (-i P_y)(-i P_y) - (T_x + \Delta_m)^2 = 0. \]

    • Exact Plücker Determinant Dispersion (semiDiracPluckerDet): The terminal Plücker minor \(p_{23}(Z_{\mathrm{SD}})\) equals the squared semi-Dirac energy \(E_{\mathrm{SD}}^2(k_x, k_y; \Delta_m)\), which vanishes at the two Dirac nodes \((T_x = -\Delta_m, P_y = 0)\) for \(\Delta_m < 0\) and at the semi-Dirac point \((0,0)\) for \(\Delta_m = 0\).

    • Perfect-Square Fubini–Study Kähler Determinant (semiDiracKahlerDetFactor): Because \(W_{\mathrm{SD}}^\dagger W_{\mathrm{SD}} = E_{\mathrm{SD}}^2\,I_2\), the Cauchy–Binet Kähler determinant factor (grassmannian-complex-gpe-igpe-plucker-madelung-paper.md) simplifies to an exact square:

      \[ \det\bigl(I_2 + W_{\mathrm{SD}} W_{\mathrm{SD}}^\dagger\bigr) = \bigl\|p(Z_{\mathrm{SD}})\bigr\|_{\mathbb{C}^6}^2 = 1 + \operatorname{Tr}(W_{\mathrm{SD}} W_{\mathrm{SD}}^\dagger) + |\det W_{\mathrm{SD}}|^2 = 1 + 2 E_{\mathrm{SD}}^2 + E_{\mathrm{SD}}^4 = \bigl(1 + E_{\mathrm{SD}}^2\bigr)^2 \ge 1. \]

  3. Extension to \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,6)\) in Uniaxially Strained Rhombohedral Trilayer Graphene (RTG, \(N=3\)): In RTG (grassmannian-rhombohedral-graphene-superconductivity-qc-paper.md), the 6-orbital sublattice space \((A_1, B_1, A_2, B_2, A_3, B_3)\) has boundary Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,6)\) (gmgsBoundaryGrassmannianRealDim 3 = 16, gmgsPluckerSublatticeQuditDim 3 = 15). 2.2 Regimes 3 & 4 in Detail: DDF Longitudinal Sheath Jamming and Anisotropic Tensor Gaussian Splats¶

Why should a physical medium exhibit non-zero inertia \(m_x^* > 0\) along \(\hat{x}\) while remaining strictly massless (\(m_y^* = 0\)) along \(\hat{y}\)?

  1. DDF Directional Shear-Thickening Jamming (MovingSheath in Signals.DDF, semiDiracDdfJammedMassX): In the Dilatant Dark Fluid (src/signals/Signals/DDF.lean), the vacuum/condensate consists of a superfluid \(\phi\) phase coupled to a dispersed \(\varphi\) phase that undergoes stress-induced dilatant jamming along the axis of compression. When a sheath moves or is strained along the \(x\)-axis at dimensionless velocity ratio \(\beta_x = v_x / c_\phi \in [0, 1)\), the longitudinal sheath compression factor is the Lorentz jamming factor \(\Lambda_\parallel(\beta_x) = \gamma_\varphi(\beta_x) = (1 - \beta_x^2)^{-1/2}\):

    • Along the compressed \(x\)-axis: Inter-particle jamming of the \(\varphi\) phase opens a Bogoliubov/jamming effective mass (semiDiracDdfJammedMassX)

      \[ m_x^*(\beta_x) = m_0\,\gamma_\varphi(\beta_x)^2 = \frac{m_0}{1 - \beta_x^2} > m_0, \qquad T_x^{\mathrm{DDF}}(\beta_x) = \frac{T_x}{1 - \beta_x^2}, \]

      which diverges as \(\beta_x \to 1^-\) (complete longitudinal localization / flat band).

    • Along the orthogonal \(y\)-axis: There is zero normal compression (\(\Lambda_\perp = 1\)), so transverse acoustic/shear excitations propagate through the superfluid \(\phi\) channel at the unjammed phonon speed \(v_y = c_\phi = (\varrho_{\max}^\varphi j_{\min}^\varphi)^{-1/2}\) with zero rest mass (\(m_y^* = 0\))!

  2. Anisotropic 2D Normed Tensor Gaussian Splat Evolution (semiDiracAnisotropicSplatVarX, semiDiracAnisotropicSplatVarY): Representing a localized semi-Dirac wavepacket as a 2D NormedTensorGaussianSplat2D (grassmannian-splat-mechanics-paper.md) with principal widths \((\sigma_{x,0}, \sigma_{y,0})\) aligned with the crystal axes \((x, y)\):

    • Along the massive \(x\)-axis, the repulsive Madelung Bohm quantum potential \(Q_x = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2 m_x^*}\frac{\partial_x^2\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\) drives standard Schrödinger wavepacket spreading (semiDiracAnisotropicSplatVarX):

      \[ \sigma_x^2(t) = \sigma_{x,0}^2 + \frac{\hbar^2 t^2}{4 (m_x^*)^2 \sigma_{x,0}^2} > \sigma_{x,0}^2 \qquad (t > 0). \]

    • Along the massless \(y\)-axis, the linear relativistic dispersion \(E_y = \hbar v_y |k_y|\) has zero second derivative (\(\frac{\partial^2 E_y}{\partial k_y^2} = 0\)), so a co-moving chiral wavepacket propagates at constant speed \(v_y\) with zero dispersive broadening (semiDiracAnisotropicSplatVarY):

      \[ \sigma_y^2(t) = \sigma_{y,0}^2 \qquad (\forall\,t \ge 0). \]

    This hybrid “spread-in-\(x\), soliton-in-\(y\)” Tensor Gaussian Splat dynamics is unique to semi-Dirac fermions and is machine-checked in semi_dirac_fermions_with_and_without_grc_and_ddf_identities.

3.2 Critique, Quantitative \(\mathrm{ZrSiS}\) \(B^{2/3}\) Benchmark vs. Isotropic Baselines, & Deep Gap Closures (Signals.DFT Section 20.2)¶

  1. Gap 1 — Exact 2D Anisotropic Density-of-States Exponent Interpolation (semiDiracDosExponent): For a general 2D quasiparticle dispersion \(E(k_x, k_y) \sim |k_x|^{p_x} + |k_y|^{p_y}\), phase-space integration \(\int dk_x\,dk_y\,\delta\bigl(E - (|k_x|^{p_x} + |k_y|^{p_y})\bigr) \propto E^{\nu_{\mathrm{DOS}}(p_x, p_y)}\) yields the exact exponent (semiDiracDosExponent):

    \[ \nu_{\mathrm{DOS}}(p_x, p_y) = \frac{1}{p_x} + \frac{1}{p_y} - 1, \qquad \nu_{\mathrm{DOS}}(2, 2) = 0 < \nu_{\mathrm{DOS}}(2, 1) = \frac{1}{2} < \nu_{\mathrm{DOS}}(1, 1) = 1. \]

  2.     \[ T_x(\kappa_x L) = \frac{1}{1 + (\kappa_x L)^2} < T_y = 1 \qquad (\kappa_x L > 0,\text{ e.g. } T_x(2) = \frac{1}{5} < 1). \]

  3.
