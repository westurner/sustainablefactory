1. The Axial Depth Bottleneck in 2D Projection Imaging and How \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Solves It¶

1.1 Why Standard 2D Projections Lose \(\sigma_3^2\) and Entangle \(z_c\)¶

Consider a 3D volumetric Gaussian feature splat : NormedTensorGaussianSplat3D (…/src/signals/Signals/DDF.lean) centered at \((x_c, y_c, z_c)\) with principal variances \((\sigma_1^2, \sigma_2^2, \sigma_3^2)\). When imaged onto a 2D detector plane \((x, y)\):

  1. Orthographic X-Ray / Radon Marginal Projection (projectOrthographic2D): Integrating along the beam axis \(z\) integrates the normalized axial Gaussian factor \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_3}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(z-z_c)^2}{2\sigma_3^2}\right)dz = 1\) to unity. The resulting 2D screen splat retains (centerX, centerY, weight, sigma1, sigma2, orientationAngle) but completely erases centerZ and sigma3.

  2. Perspective Pinhole / EWA Projection (projectPerspective2D): Projecting from bulk depth \(z_c > 0\) through focal length \(f > 0\) scales the screen coordinates and lateral widths by \(s = f / z_c\):

    \[ \sigma_{1,\mathrm{persp}} = \frac{f}{z_c}\,\sigma_1, \qquad \sigma_{2,\mathrm{persp}} = \frac{f}{z_c}\,\sigma_2. \]

    A small object close to the lens (\(z_c\) small) and a large object far from the lens (\(z_c\) large) produce identical 2D screen widths unless an independent depth channel is available.
