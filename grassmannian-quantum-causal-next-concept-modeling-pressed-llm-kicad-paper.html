Abstract¶

Autoregressive (AR) Large Language Models generate text sequentially in \(L\) steps (\(O(L)\) sequential weight-fetch memory bottlenecks), whereas discrete and continuous Diffusion Language Models (DLMs, such as MDLM [Sahoo et al., 2024] and DUO [Sahoo et al., 2025]) process the full length-\(L\) sequence at every denoising step \(t \in \{1,\dots,T\}\), forfeiting causal KV-caching and requiring \(4\text{--}5\times\) larger training-token budgets. Recently, Kumbong et al. ([Kumbong et al., 2026], arXiv:2610.14162) introduced Next-Concept Modeling (NCM), a two-stage coarse-to-fine discrete hierarchical framework in which a multiscale residual VQ-VAE encodes text into \(K \ll L\) levels of progressively longer code sequences \(\ell_1 < \ell_2 < \cdots < \ell_K = L\) and a block-diagonal transformer generates one level at a time, predicting all \(\ell_k\) codes within level \(k\) in parallel from the cumulative reconstruction \(\hat{z}^{(k-1)}\). While NCM achieves \(10\text{--}31\times\) faster wall-clock inference and \(4\text{--}16\times\) fewer function evaluations (NFE) than diffusion baselines, our mathematical and causal audit identifies four structural bottlenecks in classical NCM: (i) intra-level conditional independence (\(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell_k} p_\theta(r_k^i \mid \hat{z}^{(k-1)})\) has zero intra-level conditional mutual information \(I(r_k^i : r_k^j \mid \hat{z}^{(k-1)}) = 0\), allowing conflicting parallel choices in Hard Sudoku and named-entity/numeral substitutions), (ii) teacher-forcing exposure confounding (where the encoder latent \(z = E_\phi(x)\) acts as an unobserved backdoor confounder between \(\hat{z}^{(k-1)}\) and \(r_k\)), (iii) quadratic intra-block self-attention (\(\sum_{k=1}^K \ell_k^2 d_{\mathrm{head}}\) as \(\ell_k \to L\)), and (iv) electronic HBM weight-fetch and sequential \(M_{\mathrm{cand}} \cdot K\) Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) overhead.

In this paper, we synthesize NCM with our Grassmannian Quantum Causal Inference (QCI) (Paper 18; Paper 20; Paper 21), Pressed / Mask-Locked 1T-Parameter LHAT Holographic Tape (Paper 38), and provenance chat architectures (_Read our A2Q and quantum review chats about N-LIG and B-LIG...md, lines 105-285; _LLMs, Fourier, and Quantum Logic .md, lines 28-695; _Mpemba Effect_ Landau vs. Quantum .md, lines 395-535) to develop Quantum Causal Next-Concept Modeling (QCI-NCM and QCI-G-NCM) and its Pressed-LHAT Optoelectronic Accelerator (Pressed-NCM-QCI), machine-checked in Lean 4 (Signals.QuantumCausalInference, Section 15) and Rust/WASM (quantum_causal_inference.rs, Section 10; cannon_mask_cad.rs):

1.1 Mathematical Formulation of NCM (Kumbong et al. 2026)¶

In Next-Concept Modeling: Fast, Parallel Coarse-to-Fine Text Generation ([Kumbong et al., 2026], arXiv:2610.14162), Kumbong, Khare, Mlauzi, Woen, Ding, Buchanan, Arora, Mirhoseini, and Ré reformulate non-autoregressive language modeling around a two-stage coarse-to-fine discrete concept hierarchy:

  1. Stage 1 — Multiscale Residual VQ-VAE Tokenization with Nested Dropout: Given a token sequence \(x \in \mathcal{T}^L\) of length \(L\), an encoder \(E_\phi\) maps \(x\) to continuous latents \(z = E_\phi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}\). Across \(K \ll L\) hierarchical levels with strictly increasing sequence lengths \(\ell_1 < \ell_2 < \cdots < \ell_K = L\) and level-specific codebooks \(\mathcal{V}_k = \{e_{k,1}, \dots, e_{k,|\mathcal{V}_k|}\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d\), multiscale residual quantization initializes \(z^{(0)} = z\) and \(\hat{z}^{(0)} = 0\), and at each level \(k \in \{1, \dots, K\}\) downsamples the residual \(z^{(k-1)}\), quantizes each of the \(\ell_k\) positions by nearest-neighbor lookup, and maps the quantized vectors back to length \(L\) via a reconstruction operator \(R_k\):

    \[ u_k = \mathrm{Down}_k\bigl(z^{(k-1)}\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{\ell_k \times d}, \qquad r_k^i = \arg\min_{j \in \{1,\dots,|\mathcal{V}_k|\}} \bigl\|u_k^i - e_{k,j}\bigr\|_2^2, \]
    \[ \hat{z}^{(k)} = \hat{z}^{(k-1)} + R_k\bigl(e_k(r_k)\bigr), \qquad z^{(k)} = z - \hat{z}^{(k)}, \qquad \hat{z}^{(K)} = \sum_{k=1}^K R_k\bigl(e_k(r_k)\bigr). \]

    To force early coarse levels (\(\ell_k \ll L\)) to encode global semantic concepts rather than deferring all information to the finest level \(\ell_K = L\), NCM applies nested dropout during tokenizer training: a random cutoff level \(k \in \{1, \dots, K\}\) is sampled, and both the token reconstruction cross-entropy and VQ commitment/codebook MSE losses are evaluated on the truncated cumulative reconstruction \(\hat{z}^{(k)}\):

    \[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tok}} = -\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^L \log p_\psi\bigl(x^i \mid \hat{z}^{(k)}\bigr) + \beta\,\mathrm{MSE}\bigl(z, \mathrm{sg}[\hat{z}^{(k)}]\bigr) + \mathrm{MSE}\bigl(\mathrm{sg}[z], \hat{z}^{(k)}\bigr). \]

  2. Stage 2 — Coarse-to-Fine Parallel Level Generation with Block-Diagonal Attention: With the multiscale VQ-VAE frozen, the NCM generator transformer \(p_\theta\) models the joint prior over the discrete concept hierarchy \(r_{1:K} = (r_1, \dots, r_K)\) autoregressively across levels \(k = 1, \dots, K\), while predicting all \(\ell_k\) codes within level \(k\) conditionally independently in parallel:

    \[ p_\theta(r_{1:K}) = \prod_{k=1}^K p_\theta\bigl(r_k \mid \hat{z}^{(k-1)}\bigr) = \prod_{k=1}^K \prod_{i=1}^{\ell_k} p_\theta\bigl(r_k^i \mid \hat{z}^{(k-1)}\bigr). \]

    Because the cumulative reconstruction \(\hat{z}^{(k-1)}\) already summarizes levels \(1, \dots, k-1\), resizing and projecting \(\hat{z}^{(k-1)}\) to \(\ell_k\) positions allows the transformer to use block-diagonal attention (attending only within the \(\ell_k\) positions of level \(k\), plus the prompt prefix \(P\) in conditional generation). During training, all \(K\) levels are trained simultaneously in a single forward pass under conditioning corruption (replacing a level-dependent fraction of conditioning codes \(r_k\) with random codebook entries \(\tilde{r}_k\) to form \(\tilde{z}^{(k-1)}\) while keeping target labels \(r_k\) clean):

    \[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NCM}} = -\sum_{k=1}^K w_k \sum_{i=1}^{\ell_k} \log p_\theta\bigl(r_k^i \mid \tilde{z}^{(k-1)}\bigr), \qquad w_k = \frac{\ell_k^{-\alpha}}{\sum_{j=1}^K \ell_j^{1-\alpha}}. \]

  3. Deterministic Point-Mass Variational ELBO (Appendix A of arXiv:2610.14162): Using the deterministic nearest-neighbor assignment \(r_{1:K}^*(x)\) as a point-mass variational posterior \(q(r_{1:K} \mid x) = \mathbf{1}\{r_{1:K} = r_{1:K}^*(x)\}\) with zero entropy (\(H(q) = 0\)), the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) on the marginal text log-likelihood \(\log p_{\theta,\psi}(x)\) is exact up to the posterior KL divergence:

    \[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ELBO}}(x) = \underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^L \log p_\psi\bigl(x^n \mid \hat{z}_*^{(K)}\bigr)}_{\text{Reconstruction}} + \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^K \sum_{i=1}^{\ell_k} \log p_\theta\bigl(r_k^{*i} \mid \hat{z}_*^{(k-1)}\bigr)}_{\text{Coarse-to-Fine Generation}} \le \log p_{\theta,\psi}(x), \]
    \[ \log p_{\theta,\psi}(x) - \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ELBO}}(x) = D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q(r_{1:K} \mid x) \,\|\, p_{\theta,\psi}(r_{1:K} \mid x)\right) = -\log p_{\theta,\psi}\bigl(r_{1:K}^*(x) \mid x\bigr) \ge 0. \]
