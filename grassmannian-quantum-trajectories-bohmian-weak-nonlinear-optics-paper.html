Abstract¶

How well do competing quantum trajectory theories—and our unified Complex Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(k, n)\), Three-Channel Plücker–Madelung–QFIM (\(F_{\mu\nu} = 4\,g_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{FS}}\)), Coupled Dual-Schubert iGPE (\(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4) \times \mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)^\vee\)), and Dilatant Dark Fluid (DDF) mechanics—perform when predicting the actual paths, velocities, dwell times, and conditional phase topologies of measured quantum particles?

Across five landmark experimental arenas, standard trajectory formalisms each break down in a distinct physical regime:

  1. Standard Non-Relativistic De Broglie–Bohm Pilot-Wave Theory ([Bohm, 1952, Bohm, 1952, Bohm and Hiley, 1982, Wiseman, 2007, Kocsis et al., 2011, Mahler et al., 2016]) defines particle velocity solely from the spatial phase gradient \(\mathbf{v}_S = \frac{\mathbf{j}}{\rho} = \frac{\hbar}{m}\nabla S\). In the coupled-waveguide tunneling experiment of Sharoglazova, Puplauskis, Mattschas, Toebes, & Klaers (Nature 643, 67–72, 2025) [Sharoglazova et al., 2025, Klaers et al., 2023, Klaers et al., 2026], stationary evanescent states \(\psi(x) = t\,e^{-\kappa x}\) inside a reflective step barrier (\(V_0 = 0.538\text{ meV}\), negative local kinetic energy \(\Delta = E + \hbar J_0 - V_0 < 0\)) have vanishing phase gradient (\(\nabla S = 0\)), forcing standard Bohmian mechanics to predict \(v_S = 0\) (\(\tau_{\mathrm{dwell}}^{\mathrm{Bohm}} = \infty\), \(100\%\) relative error \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}} = 1\), or \(98.5\%\) error even with \(30\text{ km/s}\) radiative-leakage drift) whereas the measured particle speed increases with barrier depth as \(v(\Delta) = \sqrt{2|\Delta|/m} = \hbar\kappa / m \sim 2{,}000\text{ km/s}\). Moreover, at interferometric dark fringes and vortex cores (\(\rho \to 0\)), the scalar Bohmian quantum potential \(Q = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\) blows up as \(1/\rho\) ([Hirschfelder et al., 1974, Hirschfelder and Tang, 1976, Philippidis et al., 1979, Lopreore and Wyatt, 1999]).

  2. Relativistic Weak-Value & Klein–Gordon Trajectory Theory (Foo, Asmodelle, Lund, & Ralph, Nature Communications 13, 4002, 2022 [Foo et al., 2022]; [Foo et al., 2024, Foo et al., 2025]) grounds single- and two-photon trajectories operationally via \(V(x,t) = \frac{\langle\hat{k}_w\rangle}{\langle\hat{H}_w\rangle} = \frac{j_K(x,t)}{\rho_K(x,t)}\), obeying Einstein velocity addition \(V' = \frac{V - v_{\mathrm{boost}}}{1 - v_{\mathrm{boost}}V}\) and matching null geodesics of a Schwarzschild–Alcubierre metric. However, at destructive interference troughs where \(\rho_K \to 0\), the unregularized scalar Klein–Gordon ratio yields unbounded superluminal coordinate speeds (\(|V| > c\)), infinite-velocity poles (\(1 - v_{\mathrm{boost}}V = 0\)), and backward-in-time trajectories (\(\rho_K' < 0\)) requiring unphysical negative-energy Alcubierre warp matter.

  3. Open-System Quantum Trajectory Theory (QTT / MCWF / Stochastic Master Equations) ([Carmichael, 1993, Dalibard et al., 1992, Plenio and Knight, 1998]; Murch et al., Nature 2013 [Murch et al., 2013]; Roch et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 2014 [Roch et al., 2014]; Campagne-Ibarcq et al., Phys. Rev. X 2016 [Campagne-Ibarcq et al., 2016]; Santos et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 2012 [Santos et al., 2012]; Minev et al., Nature 2019 [Minev et al., 2019]) tracks internal Bloch/Hilbert-space trajectories under homodyne, heterodyne, or photon-jump monitoring (reducing \(N^2\) Lindblad density-matrix equations to \(N\) statevector equations), but lacks a real-space hydrodynamic velocity decomposition and scales exponentially as \(N = 2^n\) on multi-qubit registers unless projected onto fixed-excitation Grassmannian Plücker sectors \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(k, n)\).

  4. Time-Varying Epsilon-Near-Zero (ENZ) Diffraction (Tirole, Vezzoli, Galiffi, Pendry, & Sapienza, Nature Physics 19, 999–1002, 2023 [Tirole et al., 2023, Tirole et al., 2022, Tirole et al., 2024]) demonstrates Young’s double-slit diffraction in time on an Indium-Tin-Oxide (ITO) ENZ film, where linear equilibrium thermal models (\(\tau_r \approx 110\text{ fs}\)) fail by over an order of magnitude to predict the observed \(1\text{–}10\text{ fs}\) (\(4.4\text{ fs}\) optical-cycle) rise time and \(31\text{ THz}\) spectral fringe comb.

  5. Strongly Interacting Quantum Nonlinear Optics (Drori, Das, Zohar, Winer, Poem, Poddubny, & Firstenberg, Science 381, 193–198, 2023/2024 [Drori et al., 2023]) breaks the linear superposition principle (\(\mathcal{N}(\psi_1 + \psi_2) \neq \mathcal{N}(\psi_1) + \mathcal{N}(\psi_2)\)), nucleating two-photon vortex–antivortex pairs (\(\phi_0^{(2)} = -\pi\)) and three-photon vortex lines plus a central vortex ring at \(\mathrm{OD} \approx 80\).
