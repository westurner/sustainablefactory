1. ‘t Hooft’s and Susskind’s Holographic Principle, AdS/CFT vs. Fluid/Gravity Duality, and DDF Acoustic Horizons¶

1.1 Historical & Mathematical Foundations: From Bekenstein–Hawking to ‘t Hooft (1993) and Susskind (1995)¶

In 1973–1975, Jacob Bekenstein (Bekenstein 1973) and Stephen Hawking (Hawking 1975) discovered that the thermodynamic entropy of a black hole scales not with its 3D volume \(V \propto r_H^3\), but with the 2D surface area \(A_H = 4\pi r_H^2\) of its event horizon:

\[ S_{\mathrm{BH}} = \frac{k_B\,c^3\,A_H}{4 G\hbar} = \frac{k_B\,A_H}{4\,\ell_P^2}, \qquad \ell_P = \sqrt{\frac{G\hbar}{c^3}}. \]

Building on this area law, Gerard ‘t Hooft (1993) (Dimensional Reduction in Quantum Gravity, gr-qc/9310026) proposed that at the Planck scale, quantum gravity in \(3+1\) dimensions reduces to a \((2+1)\)-dimensional theory of Boolean/cellular degrees of freedom living on a 2D boundary surface, with information density bounded by \(\frac{1}{4\ell_P^2}\) nats per unit area. In 1995, Leonard Susskind (The World as a Hologram, J. Math. Phys. 36, 6377, hep-th/9409089) elevated ‘t Hooft’s dimensional reduction into the Holographic Principle, demonstrating via relativistic light-front / light-sheet focusing (later generalized covariantly by Bousso 2002) that any bulk configuration exceeding \(S_{\mathrm{BH}}\) inside an enclosing area \(A_H\) must collapse into a horizon of area at least \(A_H\).

In 1997, Juan Maldacena (Maldacena 1998) realized the Holographic Principle in string theory via the AdS/CFT correspondence (formalized with the Gubser–Klebanov–Polyakov–Witten mass–dimension dictionary \(\Delta_\pm = d/2 \pm \sqrt{(d/2)^2 + m^2 R_{\mathrm{AdS}}^2}\), the Kovtun–Son–Starinets viscosity bound \(\eta/s = \hbar/(4\pi k_B)\), planar \(\mathcal{N}=4\) SYM integrability Beisert et al. 2012, arXiv:1012.3982, and \(\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)_c\) secret symmetries de Leeuw et al. 2012, arXiv:1204.2366 in our companion paper AdS/CFT Correspondence, Planar Integrability, and Secret Symmetries in Unified Complex Grassmannian \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) and DDF Mechanics), and Ryu and Takayanagi (2006) showed that boundary entanglement entropy equals the minimal bulk surface area \(S_A = \frac{\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4 G_N}\).
