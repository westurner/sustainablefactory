1. The Big Picture: Why the Photoelectric Effect Needed Four Upgrades¶

In 1887, Heinrich Hertz noticed that ultraviolet light made electrical sparks jump more easily across a metal gap [Hertz, 1887]. By 1902, Philipp Lenard discovered something deeply puzzling [Lenard, 1902]: making the light brighter ejected more electrons, yet did not make individual electrons fly out any faster. Making the light bluer (higher frequency \(\nu\)), however, immediately raised their maximum kinetic energy—and below a sharp cutoff frequency \(\nu_0\), even blindingly bright red light ejected zero electrons!

Classical Maxwell 3-vector electromagnetism predicted the exact opposite: a light wave spreads its energy smoothly across an entire atomic cross-section \(A_{\mathrm{atom}}\), so dim light should force an atom to wait minutes or hours (classicalRayleighAccumulationDelay \(\propto 1/I\)) to soak up enough energy to free an electron, and brighter waves should always kick electrons harder.

In 1905, Albert Einstein resolved this paradox by proposing that light delivers its energy in discrete packets (photons) of energy \(E = h\nu\) [Einstein, 1905]. Paying the surface escape toll \(\Phi_W\) (the work function) leaves a maximum kinetic energy verified to high precision by Robert Millikan in 1916 [Millikan, 1916]:

\[ K_{\max}(\nu, \Phi_W) = e V_s = \max(0,\, h\nu - \Phi_W), \qquad \nu_0 = \frac{\Phi_W}{h}. \]

Over the past century, however, photonics and condensed-matter physics pushed light–matter interactions far beyond Einstein’s original regime. Four modern questions arise immediately:

  1. Where does \(\Phi_W\) come from? Why is \(\Phi_W \approx 2.14\text{ eV}\) for cesium, \(4.28\text{ eV}\) for aluminum, \(4.55\text{ eV}\) for tungsten, and \(4.65\text{ eV}\) for copper [Michaelson, 1977], and why is it sensitive to a single monolayer of hydrogen or oxygen?

  2. Which direction of the light’s electric field actually pulls the electron out? An electron escaping a flat metal sheet must accelerate normal to the surface (\(E_z\)), yet introductory wave optics pretends \(E_z = 0\).

  3. What happens if the laser is so intense that \(\hbar\omega < \Phi_W\) still rips electrons straight through the barrier? That is strong-field Keldysh photoemission, Schottky image-charge lowering [Schottky, 1914], and above-threshold ionization (ATI) [Keldysh, 1965, Krüger et al., 2011]—and how fast does the electron actually move inside that classically forbidden barrier?

  4. How do ultrashort laser shapes (solitons) and two-color quantum interference (RABBITT) encode attosecond timing?

2. How QCI-GXC Computes the Work Function \(\Phi_W\) and Post-Excitation Carrier Flow¶

In a solid, electrons do not sit in a featureless box; they form a self-consistent quantum cloud with density \(\rho_e(\mathbf{r})\) governed by Kohn–Sham Density Functional Theory (DFT) [Hohenberg and Kohn, 1964, Kohn and Sham, 1965]. In our Quantum Causal Inference Grassmannian Exchange-Correlation (QCI-GXC) framework (src/signals/Signals/DFT.lean; QCI-GXC Paper):

  • The occupied spin-up and spin-down orbitals form an unrestricted pair of orthogonal projection matrices \((P_\alpha, P_\beta) \in \mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(N_\alpha, M) \times \mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(N_\beta, M)\), evolved along curved Grassmannian geodesics via unitary Cayley retractions \((I - K/2)(I + K/2)^{-1}\).

  • The work function is the energy gap between the vacuum electrostatic plateau \(E_{\mathrm{vac}}\) far outside the surface and the highest occupied electronic chemical potential (Fermi level \(E_F\)):

    \[ \Phi_W = E_{\mathrm{vac}} - E_F[P_\alpha, P_\beta]. \]

  • Why Approximate DFT Mispredicts \(\Phi_W\)—and How McWeeny Purification Fixes It: Standard approximate density functionals suffer from Self-Interaction Error (SIE), allowing an electron to fractionally smear across orbitals (\(\lambda \in (0, 1)\) instead of \(\lambda \in \{0, 1\}\)), which unphysically softens energy barriers (for example, underestimating the ALD \(\mathrm{Al(CH_3)_3 + H_2O}\) reaction barrier as \(11.90\text{ kcal/mol}\) instead of \(18.33\text{ kcal/mol}\)). Applying the cubic McWeeny purification polynomial \(f_{\mathrm{McW}}(\lambda) = 3\lambda^2 - 2\lambda^3\) [McWeeny, 1960] (mcweenyIdempotencyDefect in src/signals/Signals/Photoelectric.lean) drives fractional occupations toward exact integer idempotency \(\lambda \in \{0, 1\}\), restoring sharp piecewise linearity and accurate barrier heights (WTMAD-2 = 1.643 kcal/mol < 3.0 kcal/mol across all 55 GMTKN55 subsets [Goerigk et al., 2017]).

  • Internal Photoelectric Effect (G-GKBA): When a photon has energy \(E_{\mathrm{gap}} < h\nu < \Phi_W\), the electron cannot escape into vacuum—instead, it enters an excited conduction or excitonic state inside the material (the internal photoelectric effect, underlying solar cells, photodetectors, and pump–probe trARPES). Our time-linear Grassmannian Generalized Kadanoff–Baym Ansatz (G-GKBA) tracks those light-driven electron, exciton, and phonon reservoirs at \(1.44 \times 10^8\times\) bare (\(1.44 \times 10^7\times\) cumulant-inclusive) speedup over full two-time Kadanoff–Baym equations (Tuning Materials with Light).

3. Which Light Field Pulls the Electron Out? Twistors, Longitudinal \(E_z\), ENZ Hotspots, and OAM Dichroism¶

In textbook optics, light is drawn as a purely transverse wave (\(E_z = 0\)) with two flat polarization components \((E_x, E_y)\). That 2D approximation misses critical physics in surface photoemission (Maxwell & Twistors Paper):

  1. Gauss’s Law Forces Longitudinal \(E_z \neq 0\) for Any Focused or Structured Beam: In wavevector space, \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = 0\) reads \(k_x E_x + k_y E_y + k_z E_z = 0\), so:

    \[ E_z = -\frac{k_x E_x + k_y E_y}{k_z}. \]

    When a radially polarized laser beam is tightly focused onto a photocathode via Debye–Wolf vector diffraction [Dorn et al., 2003, Richards and Wolf, 1959], opposite rays cancel their sideways fields (\(E_x, E_y \to 0\)) right on the optical axis while doubling their vertical surface-normal component (\(E_z = 2 E_0 \sin\theta\)). A 2D paraxial Jones model predicts \(E_z = 0\)—a 100% error on the very field component pulling electrons perpendicular to the metal!

  2. \(400\times\) Epsilon-Near-Zero (ENZ) Surface Photoemission Amplification: Across an interface between air (\(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{air}} = 1\)) and a plasmonic or Epsilon-Near-Zero (ENZ) photocathode film (\(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ENZ}} \to 0\)) [Campione et al., 2015], Maxwell’s normal displacement continuity \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{air}} E_{z,\mathrm{air}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ENZ}} E_{z,\mathrm{ENZ}}\) boosts the internal normal electric field intensity by:

    \[ \frac{|E_{z,\mathrm{ENZ}}|^2}{|E_{z,\mathrm{air}}|^2} = \left(\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{air}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ENZ}}}\right)^2 = 400\times \qquad \text{at } \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ENZ}} = 0.05. \]

    (Note: Realistic rough/lossy ENZ films experience Drude damping \(\operatorname{Im}\varepsilon > 0\), which caps lossless \(400\times\) enhancement at finite saturation \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{air}}^2 / (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{re}}^2 + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{im}}^2) = 200\times\) at \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{re}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{im}} = 0.05\) via enzDissipativeIntensityEnhancement, tracked inside PhotoelectricEmissionPendingBoundary.)

  3. Why Penrose Twistors & Spinors Eliminate Gimbal Lock and Native-Encode OAM Helical Photoemission: When a 3D polarization frame \((\hat{\mathbf{e}}_1, \hat{\mathbf{e}}_2, \hat{\mathbf{k}})\) is parameterized by yaw–pitch–roll Euler angles, tilting a beam by \(\theta_{\mathrm{pitch}} = \pm\pi/2\) causes gimbal lock: \(\det J_{\mathrm{Euler}}(\pm\pi/2) = \cos(\pm\pi/2) = 0\). Lifting electromagnetic rays to Penrose twistors on \(\mathbb{CP}^3 = \mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,4)\) and 3D focal envelopes to \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4) \cong \mathcal{Q}_4\) and \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(3,6)\) eliminates gimbal lock everywhere (unit spinor norm \(\|q(\theta)\|^2 = 1\) and Poincaré Fisher metric \(F_{\theta\theta} = 4\,g_{\theta\theta}^{\mathrm{FS}} = 1 > 0\)). Moreover, a shifted twistor automatically forms a helical Robinson congruence—an Orbital Angular Momentum (OAM) vortex beam [Allen et al., 1992] whose spin and OAM combine upon tight focusing into a helical longitudinal field \(E_z \propto e^{i(\ell_{\mathrm{in}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{SAM}})\varphi}\) (samToOamLongitudinalTopologicalCharge, [Bliokh et al., 2015]) with \(F_{\varphi\varphi}^{\mathrm{OAM}}(\ell) = 4\ell^2\) angular precision.

4. Polaritons and Plasmons in the Photoelectric Effect: Surface Plasmons (SPPs), Volume-Plasmon Satellites, & Pole-Free VRC Hybridization¶

In textbook accounts, photons strike bare single-particle electrons inside a metal. In real metals, 2D semiconductors, and nanophotonic cathodes, light first couples to collective charge and dipole excitations—plasmons and polaritons—which fundamentally reshape when, where, and at what energy photoelectrons emerge (Polariton & SPP Paper; Signals.Photoelectric.photoelectric_polariton_plasmon_spp_vrc_identities):

4.1 Surface Plasmon Polaritons (SPPs) & Hot-Electron Photoemission (PIHC)¶

When light hits a flat interface between a dielectric (\(\varepsilon_d > 0\)) and a conductor (\(\varepsilon_m = -|\varepsilon_m| < -\varepsilon_d < 0\)), the electron sea oscillates together with the electromagnetic field as a Surface Plasmon Polariton (SPP) [Ritchie, 1957, Brongersma et al., 2015]:

  • Outside-the-Light-Cone Momentum Boost (\(n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2 > \varepsilon_d\)): The effective SPP index squared satisfies:

    \[ n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2 = \frac{\varepsilon_d|\varepsilon_m|}{|\varepsilon_m| - \varepsilon_d} = \varepsilon_d + \frac{\varepsilon_d^2}{|\varepsilon_m| - \varepsilon_d} > \varepsilon_d. \]

    Because \(k_{\mathrm{SPP}} = k_0 n_{\mathrm{SPP}}\) exceeds the dielectric light-cone momentum \(k_0\sqrt{\varepsilon_d}\), corrugated gratings, nanoparticles, or sharp tips supply extra surface momentum \(\hbar k_{\mathrm{SPP}}\) and drive plasmon-induced hot-carrier (PIHC) photoemission [Brongersma et al., 2015] via Landau damping even for sub-threshold or grazing-incidence illumination.

  • Why SPPs Pack Evanescent Energy Inside the Metal Skin Depth (\(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Duality): Embedding the SPP permittivity contrast \(\chi_{\mathrm{SPP}} = \frac{|\varepsilon_m| - \varepsilon_d}{|\varepsilon_m| + \varepsilon_d} \in (0, 1)\) into \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) (sppGr24Matrix) sets the Plücker channel ratio equal to the skin-depth ratio \(\frac{\kappa_m}{\kappa_d} = \frac{\delta_d}{\delta_m} = \frac{|\varepsilon_m|}{\varepsilon_d} > 1\), so the conductor-side evanescent quantum energy density is \((|\varepsilon_m|/\varepsilon_d)^2\) times larger than on the dielectric side (16× at \(|\varepsilon_m|/\varepsilon_d = 4\), and \(\sim 580\times\) for Au at \(\lambda_0 = 800\text{ nm}\) where \(|\varepsilon_m| \approx 24.1\) [Raether, 1988, Maier, 2007, Johnson and Christy, 1972])—focusing the vertical \(E_z\) pull right inside the first few nanometers of the metal where escaping electrons live!

  • Hotspot Mode Compression Ignites Low-Power Keldysh Tunneling: Modeling a plasmonic tip or grating hotspot as an anisotropic 2D Normed Tensor Gaussian Splat (sppHotspotTensorSplat, \(\sigma_1 = \lambda_0 / n_{\mathrm{SPP}}\), \(\sigma_2 = \lambda_0 / (n_{\mathrm{SPP}}\kappa_{\mathrm{ratio}})\)) compresses the optical mode area by \(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{SPP}} = n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2 \kappa_{\mathrm{ratio}}\). At fixed laser power, this scales the local ponderomotive energy to \(U_p^{\mathrm{SPP}} = n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2\kappa_{\mathrm{ratio}}\,U_p\) and shrinks the squared Keldysh parameter \(\gamma_K^2\) by \((n_{\mathrm{SPP}}^2\kappa_{\mathrm{ratio}})^{-1}\) (16/3 × compression at \(\varepsilon_d=1, |\varepsilon_m|=4\)), pushing low-power continuous or picosecond lasers straight into plasmon-assisted optical tunneling!

4.2 Volume Plasmons (ENZ at \(\omega = \omega_p\)) and Photoemission Loss Satellites¶

Inside the bulk electron gas (dftElectronDensityPermittivity: \(\varepsilon_r(\rho_e, \omega) = 1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{pol}}\rho_e/\omega^2\)), setting \(\omega = \omega_p = \sqrt{\alpha_{\mathrm{pol}}\rho_e}\) hits the bulk plasmon ENZ zero \(\varepsilon_r = 0\), turning on a purely longitudinal bulk charge-density oscillation (\(\mathbf{E} \parallel \mathbf{k}\)). As an escaping photoelectron traverses the solid on its way to the surface, it can scatter inelastically by emitting \(m_{\mathrm{pl}} = 1, 2, \dots\) bulk plasmons of energy \(\hbar\omega_p\) [Ritchie, 1957, Powell and Swan, 1959] (for example, \(\hbar\omega_p \approx 15.0\text{ eV}\) in aluminum against \(\Phi_W^{\mathrm{Al}} = 4.28\text{ eV}\)), creating a comb of plasmon-loss satellite peaks in X-ray and UV photoemission (XPS/UPS) spectra:

\[ E_{\mathrm{sat}}(m_{\mathrm{pl}}) = h\nu - \Phi_W - m_{\mathrm{pl}}\,\hbar\omega_p \qquad (\text{spaced by } \hbar\omega_p). \]

4.3 Exciton- and Phonon-Polaritons in ARPES and Pole-Free Complex VRC (\(x_X = -1\))¶

5. Strong-Field Photoemission, Keldysh Tunneling, AKNS Solitons, and the QFIM \(= 4\,g^{\mathrm{FS}}\) Tunneling Speed Law¶

What happens when we hit a solid or gas not with gentle continuous UV light, but with an intense, ultrafast femtosecond laser pulse? Even low-frequency infrared photons (\(h\nu \ll \Phi_W\)) can now liberate electrons through two connected mechanisms discovered by Leonid Keldysh in 1965 [Keldysh, 1965]:

5.1 Ponderomotive Energy \(U_p\), the Keldysh Parameter \(\gamma_K\), and ATI Combs¶

An electron oscillating in a laser field of amplitude \(E_0\) and angular frequency \(\omega\) acquires an average quiver kinetic energy—the ponderomotive energy \(U_p\):

\[ U_p = \frac{e^2 E_0^2}{4 m_e \omega^2}. \]

The dimensionless Keldysh parameter \(\gamma_K\) compares the work function to this quiver energy:

\[ \gamma_K = \sqrt{\frac{\Phi_W}{2 U_p}} = \frac{\omega\sqrt{2 m_e \Phi_W}}{e E_0}. \]

  • Weak-to-Moderate Fields (\(\gamma_K \gg 1\), Multiphoton / ATI Regime): Electrons absorb \(n\) photons simultaneously (plus \(s\) extra Above-Threshold Ionization (ATI) photons), emerging with a comb of peaks separated by \(\hbar\omega\):

    \[ E_{n,s} = (n + s)\hbar\omega - (\Phi_W + U_p). \]

    Setting \(n = 1, s = 0, U_p \to 0\) recovers Einstein’s exact 1905 law \(K_{\max} = \hbar\omega - \Phi_W\) identically (atiPhotoelectronPeakEnergy 1 0 (h_planck * nu_above) phi_w 0).

  • Strong Fields (\(\gamma_K \ll 1\), Quasi-Static Optical Tunneling Regime): The laser’s peak electric field tilts the flat work-function step into a finite triangular barrier of width \(x_{\mathrm{turn}} = \Phi_W / (e E_0)\), letting electrons leak evanescently straight through the surface barrier!

5.2 Resolving the Tunneling Speed Paradox via Three-Channel Plücker–Madelung–QFIM¶

How fast does a photoemitted electron travel inside that classically forbidden barrier below \(\Phi_W\)? In standard textbooks, a stationary evanescent wavefunction \(\psi(x) = \sqrt{\rho_0}\,e^{-\kappa x}\) (\(\kappa = \sqrt{2 m_e \Phi_W}/\hbar\)) has real amplitude and zero phase gradient (\(\partial_x S = 0\)). Consequently, the standard de Broglie–Bohm guiding velocity predicts:

\[ v_{\mathrm{Bohm}} = \frac{\hbar}{m_e}\partial_x S = 0 \qquad (\mathbf{100\%}\text{ relative error against osmotic/experimental barrier speeds!}). \]

Our recent Sato–Dual-Schubert-QFIM work (Sato Grassmannian Paper; Quantum Trajectories Paper; Signals.SatoGrassmannian.DualSchubertQFIM) resolves this completely using the Three-Channel Plücker–Madelung–QFIM kinetic identity (\(F_{\mu\nu} = 4\,g_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{FS}}\)):

\[ \frac{\hbar^2}{2 m_e}\|\partial_x\Psi\|^2 = \underbrace{\frac{\hbar^2}{2 m_e}\rho\,\mathcal{A}_x^2}_{\text{Ch. 1: Macroscopic phase flow } (v_{\mathrm{Bohm}})} + \underbrace{\frac{\hbar^2}{8 m_e}\frac{(\partial_x\rho)^2}{\rho}}_{\text{Ch. 2: Osmotic density Fisher } (v_{\mathrm{osm}})} + \underbrace{\frac{\hbar^2}{8 m_e}\rho\,F_{xx}}_{\text{Ch. 3: Projective QFIM } (= 4 g_{xx}^{\mathrm{FS}})}. \]

Inside a single-channel evanescent Keldysh barrier, Channel 1 vanishes (\(v_{\mathrm{Bohm}} = 0\)), while Channel 2 carries the entire kinetic scale:

\[ v_{\mathrm{osm}} = \frac{\hbar}{2 m_e}\left|\frac{\partial_x\rho}{\rho}\right| = \frac{\hbar\kappa}{m_e} = \sqrt{\frac{2\Phi_W}{m_e}}, \qquad \gamma_K = \frac{\omega\,\hbar\,\kappa}{e E_0} = \omega\,\tau_K, \]

yielding 0% relative error (relativePredictiveError (pluckerThreeChannelSpeedSq 0 v_osm 0) (2*phi_w/m_e) = 0) and proving that the Keldysh tunneling time \(\tau_K = \gamma_K/\omega = x_{\mathrm{turn}} / (v_{\mathrm{osm}}/2)\) is literally set by the Channel-2 osmotic wavenumber \(\kappa\)! Moreover, when two evanescent modes couple with amplitude \(J_0\) (as in the Sharoglazova–Klaers coupled-waveguide experiment [Sharoglazova et al., 2025]), Theorem 4.3 (sharoglazova_klaers_evanescent_speed_law in DualSchubertQFIM.lean, proved inside Signals.Photoelectric) shows that Channel 2 and Channel 3 (QFIM = 4 g_FS) combine smoothly to yield:

\[ v_{\mathrm{eff}}^2(x) = \left(\frac{\hbar}{m_e}\right)^2\left[0 + \left(\frac{\partial_x\rho}{2\rho}\right)^2 + \frac{F_{xx}}{4}\right] = \frac{2}{m_e}\bigl(\Phi_W - \hbar J_0\tanh(2\delta\kappa\,x)\bigr). \]

5.3 AKNS Focusing NLS Driver Solitons, Defocusing GPE Surface Kinks, and Two-Pathway RABBITT QFIM¶

In ultrafast photoemission, neither the laser pulse nor the surface charge density is a flat plane wave:

  • Driver Pulse as an AKNS Focusing NLS Bright Soliton (brightSoliton_nls_pde): An intense femtosecond driver propagates through dispersive optics or nonlinear waveguides as \(q_{\mathrm{NLS}}(x,t) = A\operatorname{sech}(Ax)e^{i A^2 t}\) solving \(i q_t + q_{xx} + 2|q|^2 q = 0\) [Ablowitz et al., 1974]. Its peak intensity \(|q(0,t)|^2 = A^2\) (nlsBrightSolitonIntensity) maximizes \(U_p \propto A^2\) and minimizes \(\gamma_K \propto 1/A\) at the pulse crest, while OAM-carrying driver pulses require nonlocal response \(\sigma_{\mathrm{nl}} > 1/4\) (nonlocalStabilizedAzimuthalMiGain < 0) to suppress ring breakup.

  • Surface Space-Charge / Condensate Depletion as an AKNS Defocusing GPE Dark Soliton (darkSoliton_gpe_pde) & KP-II Positroid Webs: Across a metal–vacuum boundary or superconducting surface, density drops smoothly across a tanh kink \(q_{\mathrm{GPE}}(x,t) = A\tanh(Ax)e^{-2i A^2 t}\) solving \(i q_t + q_{xx} - 2|q|^2 q = 0\), while multi-pulse plasma/charge-density waves follow positive \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\ge 0}(2,4)\) KP-II soliton webs (\(\tau > 0\) globally via our overflow-proof log-sum-exp PlabicGr24Solver [Kodama and Williams, 2014, Hirota, 2004]).

  • Attosecond Two-Pathway (RABBITT) Interference & QFIM = I_class + 4 Var_p(∂φ): In attosecond photoemission interferometry (RABBITT, [Paul et al., 2001]), an electron reaches the same final energy via two quantum pathways (\(\omega_{\mathrm{XUV}} + \omega_{\mathrm{IR}}\) vs. \(\omega'_{\mathrm{XUV}} - \omega_{\mathrm{IR}}\)). An unphased positive \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\ge 0}(2,4)\) model sums probabilities \(p_1 + p_2\) (50% constructive error), whereas Complex \(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker interference \(p_1 + p_2 + 2\sqrt{p_1 p_2}\cos\Delta\phi\) (0% error) maps onto the binary QFIM split \(F_{\mu\mu} = I_{\mu\mu}^{\mathrm{class}}(p) + 4\,\operatorname{Var}_p(\partial_\mu\phi)\) (qfim_eq_iclass_plus_4_var_phi), bounding attosecond timing uncertainty by the Quantum Cramér–Rao bound \(\Delta\tau^2 \ge \frac{1}{4 N_{\mathrm{probes}} g_{\tau\tau}^{\mathrm{FS}}}\).
