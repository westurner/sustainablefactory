1. Relativistic Inverse Compton Scattering & \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker Channels¶

In relativistic astrophysics, laser-plasma Compton light sources (Provenance Chat Log: CNT Bandgap & Laser Compton Scattering (2026), lines 390–398), and DDF moving-sheath electrodynamics, Inverse Compton Scattering (ICS) occurs when a relativistic charged particle or shear-jammed condensate parcel moving at speed \(v = \beta c\) (\(\beta \in [0, 1)\), Lorentz/jamming factor \(\gamma_\varphi(v) = (1 - \beta^2)^{-1/2}\)) collides with a low-energy photon of frequency \(\omega_0\).

1.1 The \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker Ratio as the Exact Relativistic Doppler Upshift¶

In Signals.DDF (src/signals/Signals/DDF.lean), the canonical DDF moving-sheath twistor matrix \(M_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{DDF}}(s) \in \mathbb{C}^{2 \times 4}\) has uncrossed Plücker channel amplitudes \(w_A^{\mathbb{C}} = \Delta_{01}^{\mathbb{C}}\Delta_{23}^{\mathbb{C}}\) and \(w_B^{\mathbb{C}} = \Delta_{03}^{\mathbb{C}}\Delta_{12}^{\mathbb{C}}\) with Madelung densities:

\[ \bigl|w_A^{\mathbb{C}}(M_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{DDF}})\bigr|^2 = 1 + \beta, \qquad \bigl|w_B^{\mathbb{C}}(M_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{DDF}})\bigr|^2 = 1 - \beta. \]

In the Thomson regime (\(\gamma \hbar \omega_0 \ll m_e c^2\), negligible electron recoil), a head-on collision (\(\theta_{\mathrm{in}} = \pi\)) followed by backscattering (\(\theta_{\mathrm{out}} = 0\)) applies two successive relativistic Doppler boosts:

  1. Transformation from the laboratory frame to the moving-sheath rest frame multiplies frequency by \(\gamma_\varphi(v)(1 + \beta) = \sqrt{\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}}\),

  2. Transformation from the rest frame back to the laboratory frame multiplies frequency by a second factor of \(\gamma_\varphi(v)(1 + \beta) = \sqrt{\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}}\).

Consequently, the head-on Inverse Compton frequency upshift ratio is identically the ratio of the two uncrossed \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Plücker channel densities (MovingSheath.inverseCompton_gr24_identities in src/signals/Signals/DDF.lean):

\[ \frac{\omega_{\max}^{\mathrm{ICS}}}{\omega_0} = \gamma_\varphi(v)^2\,(1 + \beta)^2 = \frac{1 + \beta}{1 - \beta} = \frac{\bigl|w_A^{\mathbb{C}}(M_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{DDF}})\bigr|^2}{\bigl|w_B^{\mathbb{C}}(M_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{DDF}})\bigr|^2} \ge 1, \]

while the angle-averaged Inverse Compton radiated power boost over an isotropic photon bath is:

\[ \frac{P_{\mathrm{ICS}}}{P_{\mathrm{Thomson}}} = \frac{4}{3}\bigl(\gamma_\varphi(v)^2 - 1\bigr) = \frac{4}{3}\,\gamma_\varphi(v)^2\,\beta^2. \]

1.2 Klein–Nishina Quantum Recoil Suppression¶

When the incident photon energy in the electron rest frame is comparable to the electron rest mass-energy \(m_e c^2\), 4-momentum conservation \(p^\mu + \hbar k^\mu = p^{\prime\mu} + \hbar k^{\prime\mu}\) introduces the dimensionless quantum recoil parameter \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}} = \frac{2\gamma\hbar\omega_0}{m_e c^2}(1 + \beta) > 0\). In Signals.Scattering (src/signals/Signals/Scattering.lean), inverseCompton_thomson_and_recoil_identities proves:

with exact recovery of the \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Thomson ratio at \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}} = 0\).

2. Stokes & Anti-Stokes Raman Scattering, \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)\) Channel Inversion, and Raman Thermometry¶

Across our materials and sensing workflows—including inline Raman quality assurance for boron-doped CNS/lignin-vitrimer busbars (Provenance Chat Log: Aluminum & Inline Raman QA (2026), lines 1490–1589), carbon-nanotube radial-breathing and \(I_D/I_G\) strain metrology (Provenance Chat Log: CNT Bandgap & Laser Compton Scattering (2026), lines 8453–8463), and Distributed Fiber Optic Sensing (DFOS) temperature profiling (Provenance Chat Log: Fiber Optic Raman Thermometry (2026), lines 95–228)—Raman scattering couples an optical pump \(\omega_L > 0\) to an internal vibrational phonon or Bogoliubov mode \(\Omega_v \in [0, \omega_L)\):

  • Stokes Channel (\(B\), phonon creation): \(\omega_S = \omega_L - \Omega_v = \omega_L(1 - \delta_R)\),

  • Anti-Stokes Channel (\(A\), phonon absorption): \(\omega_{AS} = \omega_L + \Omega_v = \omega_L(1 + \delta_R)\),

where \(\delta_R = \Omega_v / \omega_L \in [0, 1)\) is the normalized Raman shift (ramanNormalizedShift).
