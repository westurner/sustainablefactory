### 1.1 Maxwell's Original Pre-Tensor Component & Quaternion Formulation (1865 / 1873: $20$ Equations)

In *A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field* (1865) {cite:p}`maxwell1865dynamical` and *A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism* (1873) {cite:p}`maxwell1873treatise`, Maxwell did **not** write four vector equations in $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$. Instead, Maxwell wrote **20 component equations in 20 field variables** (`maxwellOriginal1865EquationCount = 20` in {download}`src/signals/Signals/DFT.lean, lines 7990–7996 <../src/signals/Signals/DFT.lean>` and `MAXWELL_ORIGINAL_1865_EQUATION_COUNT = 20` in {download}`src/signals/numerical_adapter/src/dft_plabic_vrc.rs, lines 2165–2167 <../src/signals/numerical_adapter/src/dft_plabic_vrc.rs>`):
- $3$ magnetic induction components from the **electrotonic state** (magnetic vector potential $\mathbf{A}$): $\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$,
- $3$ electromotive intensity components including convective motion $\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}$ and scalar potential $\phi$: $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B} - \partial_t\mathbf{A} - \nabla\phi$,
- $3$ total current components adding Maxwell's displacement current to conduction current: $\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{tot}} = \mathbf{J} + \partial_t\mathbf{D}$,
- $3$ Ampère–Maxwell magnetomotive curl equations: $\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{tot}}$,
- $3$ dielectric elasticity equations: $\mathbf{D} = \varepsilon\mathbf{E}$,
- $3$ Ohm's conduction equations: $\mathbf{J} = \sigma_e\mathbf{E}$,
- $1$ Gauss electric displacement equation: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{D} = \rho_e$, and
- $1$ charge continuity equation: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} + \partial_t\rho_e = 0$ ($3+3+3+3+3+3+1+1 = 20$).

In Volume II of the 1873 *Treatise* (§11 and §618), Maxwell adopted William Rowan Hamilton's **quaternion calculus** ($\mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{R} \oplus \mathbb{R}^3$, $i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$). Acting with Hamilton's quaternion nabla operator $\nabla = i\partial_x + j\partial_y + k\partial_z$ on a pure-vector quaternion $\mathbf{F} = i F_x + j F_y + k F_z$ produces **both** the negative scalar divergence and the vector curl in a single associative product:

$$
\nabla \mathbf{F} = -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} + \nabla \times \mathbf{F} \in \mathbb{H},
$$

whose quaternion norm squared (`maxwellQuaternionNablaNormSq` in {download}`src/signals/Signals/DFT.lean, lines 8018–8025 <../src/signals/Signals/DFT.lean>`) unifies longitudinal compressional divergence and transverse rotational vorticity:

$$
\|\nabla \mathbf{F}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2 = (-\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F})^2 + |\nabla \times \mathbf{F}|^2.
$$

Crucially, Maxwell's pre-tensor formulation preserved two physical pillars that were later discarded in textbook vector electrodynamics: **(a)** the electromagnetic potentials $(\mathbf{A}, \phi)$ as physical quantities—vindicated 94 years later by the **Aharonov–Bohm effect** ($\Delta\Phi_{\mathrm{AB}} = \frac{e}{\hbar}\oint \mathbf{A}\cdot d\boldsymbol{\ell} \neq 0$ even where $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$, {cite:p}`aharonov1959significance`)—and **(b)** non-singular $\mathrm{Spin}(3) \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)$ unit-quaternion rotations.
