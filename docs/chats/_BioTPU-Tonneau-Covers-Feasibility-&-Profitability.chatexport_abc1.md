# Response: 5

To successfully disperse the highly viscous pigment retentate (containing residual carotenoids, chlorophylls, and heavy lipids) alongside rigid technical kraft lignin into the algae-based BioTPU matrix, we need a highly specific **modular twin-screw extrusion profile**. 

The main challenge here is managing the wildly different physical states of the inputs: the rigid, high-melting-point lignin ($T_g \approx 130\text{–}150^\circ\text{C}$), the viscous liquid retentate, and the shear-sensitive BioTPU base resin.

Here is the exact compounding parameter blueprint, followed by the requested FJH electricity price sensitivity analysis.

---

## 1. Twin-Screw Compounding Parameters

We will use a co-rotating twin-screw extruder with a high $L/D$ ratio (typically $40:1$ to $48:1$) to ensure complete distributive and dispersive mixing without degrading the bio-components.

### Screw Configuration & Feed Zones
*   **Zone 1–2 (Feeding & Base Melting):** Feed the primary BioTPU pellets here. Set temperatures to $170\text{–}180^\circ\text{C}$ with standard forward-conveying elements to establish a stable, fully molten polymer matrix.
*   **Zone 3 (Rigid Solids Downstream Side-Feeding):** Introduce the dry, technical kraft lignin powder via a side-feeder. 
    *   *Elements:* Deploy a series of high-shear **kneading blocks (KB45/5/24)** immediately following the injection point to break down agglomerates of rigid lignin particles into sub-micron domains within the molten TPU.
*   **Zone 4 (Liquid Injection - The Synergy Stream):** Inject the pre-warmed ($60^\circ\text{C}$ to drop viscosity), liquid pigment retentate downstream via a liquid injection port using a positive displacement pump. Injecting this *after* the lignin is dispersed ensures the lipids don't coat the dry lignin particles and cause them to slip/agglomerate.
    *   *Elements:* Use **distributive mixing elements (like turbine or neutral kneading blocks)** rather than aggressive high-shear elements. We want to gently emulsify the lipids into the TPU matrix without generating excessive viscous dissipative heat that would degrade the delicate carotenoids.
*   **Zone 7–8 (Venting & Volatiles Removal):** Apply a high vacuum ($-0.8$ to $-0.9\text{ bar}$) to flash off any residual moisture from the lignin or low-molecular-weight volatiles from the algae oil.
*   **Zone 9–10 (Die & Pelleting):** Strand-die extrusion into a water bath followed by a pelletizer.

### Thermal Profile & Processing Window
Because TPU is prone to thermal degradation and the carotenoids in the retentate will char if overheated, the temperature profile must be tightly controlled:

| Extruder Zone | Function | Target Temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$) | Range Tolerance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zone 1** | Barrel Feed | $40^\circ\text{C}$ | $\pm 5^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Zone 2** | TPU Melting | $175^\circ\text{C}$ | $\pm 3^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Zone 3** | Lignin Feeding & Shear Mix | $185^\circ\text{C}$ | $\pm 2^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Zone 4** | Retentate Liquid Injection | $180^\circ\text{C}$ | $\pm 2^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Zone 5–6** | Homogenization | $175^\circ\text{C}$ | $\pm 3^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Zone 7–8** | Vacuum Degassing | $170^\circ\text{C}$ | $\pm 4^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Zone 9–10** | Die Head | $165^\circ\text{C}$ | $\pm 2^\circ\text{C}$ |

*   **Screw Speed:** Maintain between **250 – 350 RPM**. Going higher risks excessive shear heating, pushing the local melt temperature past $200^\circ\text{C}$ where the carotenoids/chlorophylls will thermal-crack and off-gas.
*   **Specific Mechanical Energy (SME):** Target an SME window of **0.18 – 0.22 kWh/kg**.
