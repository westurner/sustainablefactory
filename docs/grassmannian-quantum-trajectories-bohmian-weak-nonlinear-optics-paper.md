## Abstract

How well do competing quantum trajectory theories—and our unified **Complex Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(k, n)$, Three-Channel Plücker–Madelung–QFIM ($F_{\mu\nu} = 4\,g_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{FS}}$), Coupled Dual-Schubert `iGPE` ($\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4) \times \mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(2,4)^\vee$), and Dilatant Dark Fluid (`DDF`) mechanics**—perform when predicting the **actual paths, velocities, dwell times, and conditional phase topologies of measured quantum particles**?

Across five landmark experimental arenas, standard trajectory formalisms each break down in a distinct physical regime:
1. **Standard Non-Relativistic De Broglie–Bohm Pilot-Wave Theory** ({cite:p}`bohm1952a,bohm1952b,bohm1982,wiseman2007grounding,kocsis2011observing,mahler2016experimental`) defines particle velocity solely from the spatial phase gradient $\mathbf{v}_S = \frac{\mathbf{j}}{\rho} = \frac{\hbar}{m}\nabla S$. In the coupled-waveguide tunneling experiment of **Sharoglazova, Puplauskis, Mattschas, Toebes, & Klaers (*Nature* 643, 67–72, 2025)** {cite:p}`sharoglazova_energyspeed_2025,klaers2023particle,klaers2025reaffirming`, stationary evanescent states $\psi(x) = t\,e^{-\kappa x}$ inside a reflective step barrier ($V_0 = 0.538\text{ meV}$, negative local kinetic energy $\Delta = E + \hbar J_0 - V_0 < 0$) have vanishing phase gradient ($\nabla S = 0$), forcing standard Bohmian mechanics to predict **$v_S = 0$ ($\tau_{\mathrm{dwell}}^{\mathrm{Bohm}} = \infty$, $100\%$ relative error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}} = 1$, or $98.5\%$ error even with $30\text{ km/s}$ radiative-leakage drift)** whereas the measured particle speed increases with barrier depth as $v(\Delta) = \sqrt{2|\Delta|/m} = \hbar\kappa / m \sim 2{,}000\text{ km/s}$. Moreover, at interferometric dark fringes and vortex cores ($\rho \to 0$), the scalar Bohmian quantum potential $Q = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}$ blows up as $1/\rho$ ({cite:p}`hirschfelder1974streamlines,hirschfelder1976vortices,philippidis1979quantum,wyatt1999quantum`).
2. **Relativistic Weak-Value & Klein–Gordon Trajectory Theory** (**Foo, Asmodelle, Lund, & Ralph, *Nature Communications* 13, 4002, 2022** {cite:p}`foo_relativistic_2022`; {cite:p}`foo2024interacting,foo2025schwarzschild`) grounds single- and two-photon trajectories operationally via $V(x,t) = \frac{\langle\hat{k}_w\rangle}{\langle\hat{H}_w\rangle} = \frac{j_K(x,t)}{\rho_K(x,t)}$, obeying Einstein velocity addition $V' = \frac{V - v_{\mathrm{boost}}}{1 - v_{\mathrm{boost}}V}$ and matching null geodesics of a Schwarzschild–Alcubierre metric. However, at destructive interference troughs where $\rho_K \to 0$, the unregularized scalar Klein–Gordon ratio yields **unbounded superluminal coordinate speeds ($|V| > c$), infinite-velocity poles ($1 - v_{\mathrm{boost}}V = 0$), and backward-in-time trajectories ($\rho_K' < 0$)** requiring unphysical negative-energy Alcubierre warp matter.
3. **Open-System Quantum Trajectory Theory (`QTT` / `MCWF` / Stochastic Master Equations)** ({cite:p}`dalibard1992wave,carmichael1993open,plenio1998quantum`; **Murch et al., *Nature* 2013** {cite:p}`murch2013observing`; **Roch et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 2014** {cite:p}`roch2014observation`; **Campagne-Ibarcq et al., *Phys. Rev. X* 2016** {cite:p}`campagne2016observing`; **Santos et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 2012** {cite:p}`santos2012quantum`; **Minev et al., *Nature* 2019** {cite:p}`minev2019catch`) tracks internal Bloch/Hilbert-space trajectories under homodyne, heterodyne, or photon-jump monitoring (reducing $N^2$ Lindblad density-matrix equations to $N$ statevector equations), but lacks a real-space hydrodynamic velocity decomposition and scales exponentially as $N = 2^n$ on multi-qubit registers unless projected onto fixed-excitation Grassmannian Plücker sectors $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(k, n)$.
4. **Time-Varying Epsilon-Near-Zero (`ENZ`) Diffraction** (**Tirole, Vezzoli, Galiffi, Pendry, & Sapienza, *Nature Physics* 19, 999–1002, 2023** {cite:p}`tirole2023doubleslit,tirole2022saturable,tirole2024shg`) demonstrates Young's double-slit diffraction in **time** on an Indium-Tin-Oxide (`ITO`) ENZ film, where linear equilibrium thermal models ($\tau_r \approx 110\text{ fs}$) fail by over an order of magnitude to predict the observed $1\text{–}10\text{ fs}$ ($4.4\text{ fs}$ optical-cycle) rise time and $31\text{ THz}$ spectral fringe comb.
5. **Strongly Interacting Quantum Nonlinear Optics** (**Drori, Das, Zohar, Winer, Poem, Poddubny, & Firstenberg, *Science* 381, 193–198, 2023/2024** {cite:p}`drori2023quantum`) breaks the linear superposition principle ($\mathcal{N}(\psi_1 + \psi_2) \neq \mathcal{N}(\psi_1) + \mathcal{N}(\psi_2)$), nucleating two-photon vortex–antivortex pairs ($\phi_0^{(2)} = -\pi$) and three-photon vortex lines plus a central vortex ring at $\mathrm{OD} \approx 80$. Here, linear optics fails by **$100\%$** ($\phi_0 = 0$), while standard pairwise Kerr nonlinearity ($\phi_{\mathrm{Kerr}}^{(3)} = \binom{3}{2}(-\pi) = -3\pi$) and pairwise McGuire 1D attraction ($E_3^{\mathrm{pair}} = 4 E_2$ {cite:p}`mcguire1964study`) fail by **$50\%$** and **$33.3\%$**, respectively, against the measured blockade-saturated values $\phi_0^{(3)} = 2\phi_0^{(2)} = -2\pi$ and $E_3 = 3 E_2$.
