2.1 Overcoming the Quantum Capacitance (\(C_Q\)) Ceiling via Semi-Dirac \(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\) Dispersion (seriesQuantumDoubleLayerCapacitance)¶

In conventional graphene and carbon-nanotube supercapacitors (Carbon Chips_ Hurdles and Future .md, lines 685-740, 945-965), sub-nanometer micropores (\(d_{\mathrm{pore}} \approx 0.7\text{–}0.9\text{ nm}\)) strip the ion solvation shell (“anomalous desolvation capacitance”), driving the geometric Helmholtz double-layer capacitance \(C_{\mathrm{EDL}} = \varepsilon_r \varepsilon_0 / d_{\mathrm{pore}}\) up to \(C_{\mathrm{EDL}} \approx 600\text{ F/g}\). However, because the low-dimensional carbon sheet has a finite electronic Density of States \(D(E_F)\) at the Fermi level, adding charge \(dQ\) requires shifting the chemical potential by \(dE_F = dQ / (e^2 D(E_F))\). This introduces the Quantum Capacitance \(C_Q = e^2 D(E_F)\) in harmonic series with \(C_{\mathrm{EDL}}\) (seriesQuantumDoubleLayerCapacitance in …/src/signals/Signals/SolitonBus.lean):

\[ \frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{tot}}(C_{\mathrm{EDL}}, C_Q)} = \frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{EDL}}} + \frac{1}{C_Q} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad C_{\mathrm{tot}}(C_{\mathrm{EDL}}, C_Q) = \frac{C_{\mathrm{EDL}}\,C_Q}{C_{\mathrm{EDL}} + C_Q}. \]

In unstrained 2D monolayer graphene, the massless Dirac dispersion \(E_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\mathbf{k}) = \hbar v_F |\mathbf{k}|\) yields a linear density of states \(D_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\varepsilon) = \frac{2|\varepsilon|}{\pi \hbar^2 v_F^2}\) that vanishes at the Dirac point (\(\varepsilon \to 0\)). At moderate doping (\(C_{Q,\mathrm{Dirac}} = 200\text{ F/g}\)), quantum capacitance chokes the total electrode capacitance to:

\[ C_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{Dirac}}(600, 200) = \frac{600 \times 200}{600 + 200} = \mathbf{150\text{ F/g}}, \]

wasting \(75\%\) of the \(600\text{ F/g}\) double-layer capacity. By applying \(532\text{ nm} / 1064\text{ nm}\) Laser Compression Shock (LCS) strain engineering from Paper 29 and Paper 31 to merge the Dirac cones into an anisotropic Semi-Dirac node (\(E_{\mathrm{SD}}(\mathbf{k}) = \sqrt{(\hbar^2 k_x^2 / 2m_x^*)^2 + (\hbar v_y k_y)^2}\), where the DOS exponent flips from \(+1\) to \(-1/2\), \(D_{\mathrm{SD}}(\varepsilon) \propto \sqrt{m_x^*}/(v_y \sqrt{\varepsilon})\)) coupled with covalently grafted quinone flat-band states (\(C_{Q,\mathrm{SD}} = 1800\text{ F/g}\)), the series capacitance triples (seriesQuantumDoubleLayerCapacitance 600 1800 = 450 = 3 * seriesQuantumDoubleLayerCapacitance 600 200):

\[ C_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{SD}}(600, 1800) = \frac{600 \times 1800}{600 + 1800} = \mathbf{450\text{ F/g}} = 3 \times C_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{Dirac}}(600, 200). \]

2.4 Relative Potential Efficiency of Processing Hemp & Flax Bast Fiber with Hydroxyl Radical (\(\cdot\mathrm{OH} / \mathrm{OH}^*\)) Cold Plasma Instead of Pyrolysis + \(\mathrm{KOH}\) Chemical Activation (hydroxylPlasmaSpecificEnergyEfficiencyRatio, bastActivationVolumetricConductiveRateFoM)¶

Across the chat corpus (Carbon Chips_ Hurdles and Future .md, lines 789-820, 930-985, Lignin-Derived-Carbon-Fibers-Applications.md, lines 155-395, _Sustainable textiles, outerwear, apparel .md, lines 3500-3515, 4226-4255, 4850-4925, _Sustainable Composites_ Energy, Processing, Costs .md, lines 990-1045, and frequencies-of-hydroxyl-plasma.md, lines 15-85), two distinct pathways are evaluated for transforming raw decorticated hemp or flax bast fiber into hierarchically porous supercapacitor and battery electrodes:

  1. Pathway A — Conventional Wet Hydrothermal Autoclave + \(800^\circ\text{C}\) \(\mathrm{KOH}\) Pyrolysis + \(\mathrm{HCl}\) Acid Wash (Mitlin Baseline):

    • Step A1 (Hydrothermal Retting / Stabilization): Raw hemp bast fiber is autoclaved in deionized water at \(180\text{–}200^\circ\text{C}\) for \(24\text{ hours}\) (or thermally stabilized at a slow \(0.5\text{–}2^\circ\text{C/min}\) ramp to \(250^\circ\text{C}\)) to dissolve hemicellulose and crosslink lignin into hydrochar (\(\sim 45\%\) mass yield).

    • Step A2 (High-Temperature \(\mathrm{KOH}\) Bulk Chemical Etching): The dried hydrochar is impregnated with corrosive potassium hydroxide at a \(1:1\) to \(4:1\) \(\mathrm{KOH}:\mathrm{C}\) mass ratio and pyrolyzed at \(700\text{–}900^\circ\text{C}\) for \(1\text{–}3\text{ hours}\) under \(\mathrm{N}_2/\mathrm{Ar}\). Metallic potassium intercalates and violently gasifies the carbon lattice from the inside out:

      \[ 6\mathrm{KOH} + 2\mathrm{C} \longrightarrow 2\mathrm{K} + 3\mathrm{H}_2\uparrow + 2\mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{CO}_3, \qquad \mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{CO}_3 \longrightarrow \mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{O} + \mathrm{CO}_2\uparrow. \]

    • Four Structural & Thermodynamic Penalties of \(\mathrm{KOH}\) Activation:

      • Bulk Carbon Burn-Off (\(Y_{\mathrm{KOH}} \approx 18\%\)): Consuming \(2\text{ mol }\mathrm{C}\) per \(6\text{ mol }\mathrm{KOH}\) burns away \(50\text{–}70\%\) of the carbonized fiber mass, reducing net raw-bast-to-electrode carbon yield to \(Y_{\mathrm{KOH}} \approx 18\%\).

      • Catastrophic Electrical Conductivity Collapse (\(\sigma_{\mathrm{KOH}}: 80 \to 30 \to 5\text{ S/cm}\)): As documented in the activation trade-off dataset (Lignin-Derived-Carbon-Fibers-Applications.md, lines 324-333), raising \(\mathrm{KOH}\) activation intensity from \(10\% \to 50\% \to 90\%\) expands BET surface area from \(500 \to 1{,}500 \to 2{,}500\text{ m}^2/\text{g}\), but unzips the long-range \(sp^2\) graphitic backbone, collapsing electrical conductivity by \(16\times\) (from \(80\text{ S/cm}\) down to \(5\text{ S/cm}\)) and shattering the fibers into a low-density powder (\(\rho_{\mathrm{KOH}} \approx 0.35\text{ g/cm}^3\)).

      • Thermal Volatilization of Pseudocapacitive Dopants: The \(800^\circ\text{C}\) furnace dwell burns off the very edge pyridinic/pyrrolic nitrogen (-NH-/=N-) and quinone oxygen (=O) functional groups needed for Faradaic pseudocapacitance.

      • High Process Energy (\(E_{\mathrm{KOH}} \approx 20\text{ kWh/kg}\)): Combining the \(24\text{ hr}\) autoclave, \(800^\circ\text{C}\) furnace, drying heat, and embodied \(\mathrm{KOH}/\mathrm{HCl}\) reagent energy consumes \(\sim 20\text{ kWh/kg}\) of finished carbon (\(\approx 111.1\text{ kWh}\) per kg of finished carbon normalized to raw bast yield, or \(20 / 0.18\text{ kWh/kg-product}\)).
