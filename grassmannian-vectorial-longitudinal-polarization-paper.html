Abstract¶

However, when a radially polarized Continuous Wave (CW) laser beam is tightly focused at high Numerical Aperture (\(\mathrm{NA} = n\sin\theta > 0.9\), Richards & Wolf 1959; Dorn, Quabis, & Leuchs 2003; Vectorial Wave Model Chat 2026), non-paraxial ray bending tilts the electric field into a 3-element vector \(\mathbf{E}(r, \phi, z) = (E_x, E_y, E_z)^T \in \mathbb{C}^3\) whose transverse components cancel on-axis while its longitudinal component \(E_z\) interferes constructively to form a sub-diffraction focal needle.

In this paper, we review, critically evaluate, and machine-check in Lean 4 (Signals.DDF and Signals.Geometry, with full LeanArchitect @[blueprint] annotations) and Rust/WASM (orbital_splat.rs) the complete optical physics of data/chats/Vectorial-Wave-Model-for-Longitudinal-Polarization.md, unifying it with higher-rank complex Grassmannian \(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(3, n)\) (\(Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(3, 6)\)) 3D volumetric imaging:

Because 2D paraxial Jones calculus predicts \(E_{z,\mathrm{2DJones}} = 0\), it suffers a \(100\%\) relative error on the longitudinal focal intensity for all \(\sin\theta \ne 0\).
